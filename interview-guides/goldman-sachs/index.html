Goldman Sachs Interview Guide (2026)

Goldman recruits engineers, quant/strats, and division analysts, and each track has a distinct bar, but all share an emphasis on rigor, ownership, and being coachable. Engineering interviews cover data structures, complexity, and practical design; markets and banking interviews probe why-Goldman, why-this-desk, and comfort with numbers under time pressure. Interviewers repeatedly test whether you can explain your reasoning cleanly, not just reach an answer.

Round-by-round: exactly what's asked & how to prepare

1

Online Assessment / HackerRank

~60–90 min
Coding and aptitude fundamentals

Timed coding problems plus, for some tracks, numerical and logical reasoning sections. Engineering candidates usually get 1–3 algorithmic questions of easy-to-medium difficulty. Partial correctness and passing edge-case tests both matter, so budget time to test your own code.

Example questions
  • Array and string manipulation with tight time limits
  • Hash-map counting and frequency problems
  • Basic dynamic programming or greedy optimization
  • Numerical reasoning: percentages, ratios, sequences
  • Reading a spec carefully and handling edge cases
How to prepare
  • Grind easy/medium arrays, strings, and hashing on LeetCode and HackerRank
  • Practice untimed first, then simulate the clock to build speed
  • Review Big-O so you can pick the right approach fast
  • Use free NeetCode roadmap videos for pattern recognition
Common mistakes
  • Optimizing prematurely and running out of time before a working solution
  • Skipping self-tests and losing hidden edge-case points
What they look for
  • Working, tested solution within the window
  • Sensible time/space complexity for the constraints
2

HireVue Behavioral (recorded)

~20–30 min
Motivation and communication

Pre-recorded video answers to behavioral and motivational prompts with short prep timers. You cannot re-record most questions, so structure matters. Interviewers look for genuine interest in Goldman and the specific division, plus concise storytelling.

Example questions
  • Why Goldman Sachs and why this division specifically
  • A time you handled failure or a hard deadline
  • Working in a team with a difficult stakeholder
  • What you know about a recent market or firm development
How to prepare
  • Draft 6–8 STAR stories and rehearse them out loud on camera
  • Learn Goldman's divisions and one current firm initiative
  • Practice speaking to a webcam for 60–90 seconds cleanly
  • Use free STAR-method guides from The Muse or MIT Careers
Common mistakes
  • Generic why-Goldman answers that could apply to any bank
  • Rambling past the timer instead of a tight structured answer
What they look for
  • Specific, informed reasons for wanting the role
  • Calm, structured delivery under a countdown
3

Superday Technical

~45 min each, several
DSA, complexity, and applied design

Live coding and technical discussion with engineers. Expect medium-difficulty algorithm problems, follow-ups on complexity and trade-offs, and questions on your projects and chosen languages. For quant/strats, expect probability and math mixed in. Communication while coding is graded as heavily as the final answer.

Example questions
  • Trees, graphs, and traversal-based problems
  • Two-pointer and sliding-window optimizations
  • Deep-dive on a past project's design decisions
  • Language-specific internals (Java, Python, or C++)
  • Basic OOP and simple system/data modeling
How to prepare
  • Solve LeetCode mediums across trees, graphs, and DP
  • Prepare a 2-minute deep explanation of your best project
  • Practice narrating your thought process while coding
  • Review your resume's tech so you can defend every line
Common mistakes
  • Coding in silence and leaving the interviewer guessing
  • Bluffing on a topic instead of admitting the gap
What they look for
  • Clear reasoning aloud and responsiveness to hints
  • Solid grasp of your own resume and projects
4

Superday Behavioral / Fit

~30–45 min
Culture fit and ownership

Conversational interviews with senior staff on motivation, teamwork, ethics, and how you handle ambiguity. Goldman weighs commercial awareness and integrity heavily. Expect to explain long-term goals and why this specific team, with realistic follow-ups.

Example questions
  • Leadership or initiative in a group setting
  • Handling an ethical gray area or a mistake honestly
  • Career goals and how the role fits them
  • Curiosity about markets, products, or technology
How to prepare
  • Map each core value to a concrete story from your life
  • Prepare 3 thoughtful questions for interviewers
  • Read the firm's culture and business principles pages
  • Follow one free markets newsletter for current context
Common mistakes
  • Answers that show no research into the specific team
  • Overclaiming individual credit for team wins
What they look for
  • Authentic ownership and self-awareness
  • Commercial curiosity beyond just the paycheck

What to master

  • Data structures and algorithms
  • Time and space complexity
  • Object-oriented design basics
  • SQL and data modeling
  • Probability and quantitative reasoning
  • Behavioral / STAR storytelling
  • Markets and commercial awareness
  • Your own resume and projects

Eligibility

Role-based: analyst/engineering programs target final-year students and recent grads with strong academics; experienced hires need relevant domain and tech depth. Specific degree filters vary by division and region.

How the package is structured

  • Comp = base + discretionary bonus; bonus is the real driver and is highly year-dependent.
  • Ladder is Analyst→Associate→VP→ED→MD; each step roughly doubles total comp.
  • Senior comp includes deferred equity that vests over years (retention hook).
  • Front-office/trading/IBD pay above engineering; engineering (Strats/Eng) trails markets slightly.

Indicative 2026 market ranges aggregated from public sources (levels.fyi, Glassdoor, AmbitionBox, candidate reports). Compensation varies widely by location, team, level calibration, and negotiation — use these as directional benchmarks, not guarantees.

Your prep plan

  • Day 4–6: Grind LeetCode mediums and time yourself on OA-style sets
  • Day 7–8: Write and rehearse 8 STAR stories on camera
  • Day 9–10: Study Goldman's divisions and one current firm topic
  • Day 11–12: Do full mock technical interviews narrating aloud
  • Day 13–14: Review complexity, prep questions, and rest before superday
  • Re-run your 6 strongest LeetCode patterns and re-time an OA set
  • Rehearse why-Goldman and 4 top STAR stories out loud
  • Skim divisions, culture principles, and one recent headline

Frequently asked questions

Do I need finance knowledge for an engineering role?

No deep finance is required, but showing curiosity about how the tech supports the business is a real differentiator.

How important is the HireVue?

It is a genuine filter. Treat it like a live round: structured answers, specific reasons for Goldman, and calm delivery.

Is the coding bar FAANG-level?

Slightly below the hardest big-tech loops on raw difficulty, but clean communication and correctness under time still matter a lot.
