Zensar Interview Guide (2026)

Zensar Technologies selects freshers through an online test (aptitude plus a coding/technical section), a group discussion, a technical interview, and an HR round. It applies its 60% criterion at each academic level individually and expects sound coding fundamentals, with a GD step that makes communication a formal filter.

Round-by-round: exactly what's asked & how to prepare

1

Online Test

~60-90 min
Aptitude and coding/technical

Covers quantitative aptitude, logical reasoning, verbal ability, and a coding or technical section. Serves as the first elimination filter and expects genuine coding fundamentals.

Example questions
  • Quantitative aptitude and DI
  • Logical reasoning: series and puzzles
  • Verbal ability and comprehension
  • Coding problem on arrays/strings
  • Technical MCQs (DS, OOP, DBMS)
How to prepare
  • Take timed aptitude mocks
  • Solve easy-medium coding problems
  • Revise DS, OOP, and DBMS MCQs
  • Practice reasoning and verbal daily
Common mistakes
  • Under-preparing coding
  • Poor time management
  • Weak verbal
What they look for
  • Balanced aptitude accuracy
  • Working coding ability
  • Solid fundamentals
2

Group Discussion

15-20 min
Communication and collaboration

A GD assessing how clearly you articulate ideas, listen, and collaborate. Being able to structure your points and engage constructively matters more than dominating.

Example questions
  • Current-affairs or general topic
  • Abstract or opinion topics
  • Balancing contribution and listening
  • Structuring arguments clearly
  • Constructive disagreement
How to prepare
  • Practice speaking clearly on general topics
  • Learn to enter and structure a GD
  • Follow current affairs
  • Practice active listening
Common mistakes
  • Dominating or staying silent
  • Being aggressive
  • Speaking without substance
What they look for
  • Clear articulation
  • Active listening
  • Structured thinking
3

Technical Interview

20-45 min
Coding, DSA, and fundamentals

Tests sound coding principles, data structures and algorithms, CS fundamentals, and your projects. Zensar expects a solid technical pedigree, so DSA and clean code matter.

Example questions
  • DSA problem discussion or coding
  • OOP and one language in depth
  • DBMS and SQL
  • OS basics
  • Project walkthrough
How to prepare
  • Practice medium DSA and clean code
  • Revise OOP, DBMS, and OS
  • Prepare project explanations
  • Do a mock technical interview
Common mistakes
  • Weak DSA when directly tested
  • Overstating resume skills
  • Poor project ownership
What they look for
  • Sound coding and DSA
  • Solid fundamentals
  • Genuine project depth
4

HR Interview

10-20 min
Fit and logistics

Covers motivation, communication, relocation, and joining. Usually the final, lighter step.

Example questions
  • Tell me about yourself
  • Why Zensar
  • Relocation and flexibility
  • Strengths, weaknesses, goals
  • Comfort with standard terms
How to prepare
  • Prepare a concise self-introduction
  • Research Zensar's business
  • Have consistent flexibility answers
  • Keep documents ready
Common mistakes
  • Generic answers
  • Inconsistency across rounds
  • Appearing inflexible
What they look for
  • Clear communication
  • Genuine motivation
  • Flexibility

What to master

  • Quantitative aptitude and DI
  • Logical reasoning
  • Verbal ability and GD skills
  • Core DSA and clean coding
  • One programming language and OOP
  • DBMS and SQL
  • Operating systems basics
  • Resume project depth

Eligibility

60% or above in Class X, Class XII, and B.Tech, applied at each level individually (not just final aggregate). Candidates with backlogs are usually filtered at resume shortlisting before the online test. Exact criteria vary by drive.

Your prep plan

  • Days 1-3: Aptitude diagnostic and DSA fundamentals
  • Days 4-6: Coding practice plus reasoning/verbal
  • Days 7-8: GD practice and current-affairs reading
  • Days 9-10: Full online-test-style mock
  • Days 11-12: DSA, DBMS, OS revision and project prep
  • Days 13-14: Mock technical and HR interviews
  • Day 1: Aptitude mock plus 8-10 coding problems
  • Day 2 morning: GD practice and project prep
  • Day 2 afternoon: Mock technical round and HR rehearsal

Frequently asked questions

How is the 60% criterion applied?

At each academic level individually (10th, 12th, and B.Tech), not just as a final aggregate, so a shortfall at any level can filter you out.

Do backlogs disqualify me?

Candidates with backlogs are usually filtered at the resume shortlisting stage, before the online test. Confirm per drive.

Is there a group discussion?

Yes, Zensar commonly includes a GD as a formal round, so practice structured speaking and active listening.

How coding-heavy is Zensar?

It expects sound coding principles and DSA, both in the online test's coding section and the technical interview.

How many rounds are there?

Typically four: online test, group discussion, technical interview, and HR round.

Which DSA topics should I prioritize?

Arrays, strings, hashing, stacks/queues, sorting/searching, recursion, and basic dynamic programming.
