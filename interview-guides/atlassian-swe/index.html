Atlassian SWE Interview Guide (2026)

Atlassian is unusually transparent, publishing official craft-specific interview guides. The loop typically includes a coding round (often a take-home style or live medium), one or two craft rounds covering system and code design, a dedicated and decisive Values interview, and a hiring-manager chat. You choose your language; how you think matters most.

Round-by-round: exactly what's asked & how to prepare

1

Coding Round

~60 min
Clean, correct implementation

A LeetCode-style medium from Atlassian's bank, in your language of choice. Sometimes framed as a take-home with a follow-up discussion. Clean code and clear thinking are weighted over cleverness.

Example questions
  • Array/string manipulation with clean design
  • Tree or graph traversal
  • Hash-map based aggregation
  • Design a small data structure
  • Refactor or extend a given solution
How to prepare
  • Read Atlassian's official engineering interview guide (free on their careers site)
  • Practice mediums with an emphasis on readable code
  • Rehearse explaining thinking clearly
  • Do mock live-coding sessions
Common mistakes
  • Messy code even if correct
  • Not clarifying requirements first
  • Skipping complexity discussion
What they look for
  • Readable, well-structured code
  • Clear reasoning process
  • Solid edge-case handling
2

Craft: Code Design / LLD

~60 min
Object-oriented, extensible design

A code-design (low-level design) round where you model a small system with clean abstractions, extensibility, and good OOP. The drawing/coding tool is your choice.

Example questions
  • Design a task list or job scheduler
  • Model an extensible notification system
  • Design a small rules/permission engine
  • Build a parking-lot-style OOP model
  • Extend a design when new requirements appear
How to prepare
  • Study SOLID principles and common design patterns
  • Practice OOP modeling problems (task list, scheduler)
  • Rehearse justifying class boundaries and abstractions
  • Read the craft guide for LLD expectations
Common mistakes
  • Over-engineering with unnecessary patterns
  • Rigid design that can't extend
  • Ignoring interfaces and separation of concerns
What they look for
  • Clean, extensible abstractions
  • Justified design decisions
  • Adapts design to new requirements
3

Craft: System Design (HLD)

~60 min
Architecture and trade-offs

A system design round (for relevant levels) in the drawing tool of your choice. You scope, propose an architecture, and defend trade-offs; example prompts include a task list or job scheduler at scale.

Example questions
  • Design a distributed job scheduler
  • Design a collaborative document or issue-tracking backend
  • Notification/webhook delivery at scale
  • Search over large project data
  • Rate limiting and API design
How to prepare
  • Study system-design-primer (free)
  • Practice scoping to bottlenecks to trade-offs
  • Learn queues, caching, and consistency basics
  • Do 2 mock designs (scheduler, notifications)
Common mistakes
  • Designing before clarifying scale/requirements
  • No consistency or failure handling
  • Unjustified technology choices
What they look for
  • Structured, requirement-driven design
  • Clear trade-off reasoning
  • Awareness of failure modes
4

Values Interview

~45-60 min
Structured, scored values alignment

A dedicated, structured, and scored round on Atlassian's five values (Open company no BS; Build with heart and balance; Don't mess the customer; Play as a team; Be the change you seek). Candidates routinely underestimate it; it can be decisive.

Example questions
  • A time you were radically transparent
  • Putting the customer first under pressure
  • Balancing quality with pace ('heart and balance')
  • Driving change you believed in
  • A team-first decision over personal credit
How to prepare
  • Study Atlassian's five values and map a story to each
  • Write specific STAR examples, not generic ones
  • Practice concise, honest, self-aware answers
  • Prepare questions that reflect the values
Common mistakes
  • Treating it as a soft, unscored chat
  • Generic stories not tied to specific values
  • Answers that contradict a value (e.g., cutting corners on customer)
What they look for
  • Authentic alignment with specific values
  • Concrete, self-aware stories
  • Customer- and team-first instincts
5

Hiring Manager

~45 min
Role fit, motivation, and depth

A conversation with the hiring manager on your background, project depth, and motivation, plus practical questions about how you operate.

Example questions
  • Deep dive on a project you led
  • How you handle ambiguity and prioritization
  • A tough trade-off you made
  • Collaboration and feedback style
  • Why Atlassian and this team
How to prepare
  • Prepare a crisp project deep-dive
  • Have 4-5 STAR stories ready
  • Research the team and Atlassian's products
  • Prepare thoughtful questions
Common mistakes
  • Shallow project narratives
  • No specific motivation for Atlassian
  • Not asking any questions
What they look for
  • Clear ownership and depth
  • Genuine interest in the team
  • Mature collaboration style

What to master

  • Arrays, strings, trees, graphs, hashing
  • SOLID and OOP design patterns
  • Low-level/code design modeling
  • System design fundamentals (queues, caching, consistency)
  • Atlassian's five values
  • STAR storytelling
  • Clean, readable coding
  • Trade-off communication

Eligibility

New grad through senior; craft rounds (LLD and/or HLD) scale with level. The Values round applies to everyone and is scored.

Your prep plan

Frequently asked questions

Is the Values round really that important?

Yes. It's structured, scored, and can be decisive. Prepare specific stories mapped to each of Atlassian's five values.

Is coding take-home or live?

It can be either; sometimes a take-home with a follow-up discussion, sometimes a live medium. Either way, clean, readable code matters.

Can I pick my language?

Yes, Atlassian lets you choose your language and tools. They care most about how you think.

Where can I find official prep material?

Atlassian publishes craft-specific interview guides on its careers site, which are worth reading before your loop.

What's the difference between the craft rounds?

Code design (LLD) tests OOP modeling and extensibility; system design (HLD) tests architecture and scale trade-offs. Which you get depends on level and role.

How long is the process?

Typically about 3-6 weeks across five stages.
