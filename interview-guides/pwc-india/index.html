PwC (India) Interview Guide (2026)

PwC India (including its Acceleration/AC delivery centers) runs one of the more multi-stage fresher funnels: an online assessment, often a game-based test, then technical, HR and sometimes a partner or director round. Numerical, verbal and situational judgement dominate the assessment; technical depth is fundamentals-level for tech tracks.

Round-by-round: exactly what's asked & how to prepare

1

Online Assessment

~30-60 min
Numerical, verbal, inductive reasoning and situational judgement

An SHL-style test with numerical reasoning (tables, charts, ratios), verbal reasoning (true/false/cannot-say from a passage), inductive/abstract reasoning, and a situational judgement section mapped to PwC's values. Some drives still use the game-based 'Career Unlocked' format.

Example questions
  • Numerical reasoning from a chart or ratio table
  • True/false/cannot-say verbal inference
  • Abstract shape-sequence pattern completion
  • Situational judgement aligned to firm values
  • Timed accuracy on data-heavy items
How to prepare
  • Practice SHL-style numerical and verbal tests (free samples on SHL/AssessmentDay)
  • Drill inductive/abstract reasoning patterns
  • Read PwC's values so SJT answers align
  • Take one timed mock to build speed
Common mistakes
  • Treating verbal 'cannot say' as false
  • Rushing numerical items and misreading the table
  • Answering SJT with what you'd really do rather than the professional ideal
What they look for
  • High accuracy under time pressure
  • Correct cannot-say verbal logic
  • Value-aligned SJT choices
2

Game-Based Assessment (some drives)

~20-30 min
Cognitive traits via interactive games

Where used, a set of gamified tasks measures processing speed, risk approach, memory and attention. There are no 'right' answers in the classic sense; consistency and calm focus matter.

Example questions
  • Fast pattern and memory tasks
  • Risk-and-reward decision games
  • Attention and reaction tasks
  • Consistency across mini-games
How to prepare
  • Try free gamified cognitive assessment samples
  • Practice staying calm under fast timers
  • Sleep well and test in a quiet setting
  • Read up on how gamified tests score traits
Common mistakes
  • Panicking at the speed of the games
  • Overthinking games that measure instinct
  • Poor test environment causing errors
What they look for
  • Steady, consistent performance
  • Calm focus under speed
  • Balanced risk decisions
3

Technical Interview

~30-45 min
CS fundamentals, coding and resume depth

For tech roles, expect C++/Java, OOP, DSA, DBMS/networking basics, easy-to-medium coding, occasional puzzles, and .NET or stack-specific questions depending on the role. Consulting/audit tracks probe domain fundamentals instead.

Example questions
  • Easy-to-medium coding or logic problem
  • OOP and DBMS fundamentals
  • A puzzle or estimation question
  • Project walkthrough and your contribution
  • Basics of a technology on your resume
How to prepare
  • Solve easy-medium coding problems on LeetCode/HackerRank
  • Revise OOP, DBMS and networking basics
  • Practice a few classic logic puzzles
  • Rehearse resume projects deeply
Common mistakes
  • Freezing on a puzzle instead of thinking aloud
  • Weak fundamentals behind listed skills
  • Unclear project ownership
What they look for
  • Structured problem-solving out loud
  • Correct fundamentals
  • Ownership of projects
4

HR / Partner Round

~20-40 min
Communication, motivation and executive presence

HR screens communication and confidence; a manager/AD/director may add a deeper technical or role-fit layer; and in some tracks a partner round tests poise and presence of mind. Higher rounds reward calm, structured, professional communication.

Example questions
  • Why PwC and why this line of service
  • A leadership or teamwork story
  • Handling ambiguity or a tough client
  • Career goals and relocation/shift readiness
  • A quick, confident answer to an unexpected prompt
How to prepare
  • Research PwC's lines of service and values
  • Prepare STAR stories for teamwork and pressure
  • Rehearse concise, confident answers
  • Prepare honest relocation/shift responses
Common mistakes
  • Rambling under partner-round pressure
  • Generic motivation answers
  • Losing composure on curveballs
What they look for
  • Calm, structured communication
  • Authentic, value-aligned motivation
  • Presence of mind under pressure

What to master

  • Numerical and inductive reasoning
  • Verbal reasoning (cannot-say logic)
  • Situational judgement and PwC values
  • OOP, DBMS and networking basics
  • Easy-medium DSA/coding
  • Puzzles and estimation
  • Resume project depth
  • STAR and executive-presence communication

Eligibility

Typically 60% across 10th, 12th and graduation with no active backlogs for engineering tracks; consulting, audit and tax hire broader streams. Strong aptitude and communication matter more than prior experience for freshers. Confirm cutoffs per drive.

Your prep plan

  • Days 1-3: SHL-style numerical and verbal reasoning
  • Days 4-5: inductive reasoning plus SJT with PwC values
  • Days 6-8: easy-medium coding and OOP/DBMS revision
  • Days 9-10: puzzles and one game-based sample
  • Days 11-12: rehearse projects plus 'Why PwC'
  • Days 13-14: mock technical plus HR/partner-style round
  • One SHL-style numerical/verbal timed mock
  • Revise OOP/DBMS and solve a few easy coding problems
  • Rehearse projects, 'Why PwC' and one curveball answer

Frequently asked questions

What is the game-based assessment?

A gamified cognitive test (sometimes 'Career Unlocked') measuring traits like speed, memory and risk approach. Some drives use it, others use SHL-style tests.

How do I handle 'cannot say' verbal questions?

Choose 'cannot say' when the passage gives no basis to judge the statement true or false. Do not infer beyond the text.

Is there really a partner round?

For some tracks, yes. A partner or director round tests confidence, presence of mind and role fit, usually after HR and technical.

How technical is the tech interview?

Fundamentals plus easy-medium coding, OOP, DBMS and stack-specific basics. It is not competitive DSA.

How should I answer the SJT?

Pick the most professional, value-aligned response, not necessarily what you'd instinctively do.

How many rounds total?

Often four to five: online assessment, sometimes a game-based test, technical, HR, and in some tracks a partner round.
