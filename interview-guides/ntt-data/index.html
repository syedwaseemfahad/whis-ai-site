NTT Data Interview Guide (2026)

NTT Data runs volume fresher hiring for graduate trainee and engineer roles across delivery centers in India. The bar is fundamentals over depth: clear a well-rounded aptitude test, then show solid CS basics and honest project knowledge. Communication and reliability matter as much as raw coding.

Round-by-round: exactly what's asked & how to prepare

1

Online Assessment

~90–120 min
Aptitude, reasoning, verbal, basic coding

A long, sectional test spanning quantitative ability, logical reasoning, English, and computer programming. Sectional cutoffs mean you cannot skip a weak area, and there is typically no negative marking so attempt everything. Time pressure is the main challenge, not difficulty.

Example questions
  • Time-and-work, percentages, ratios, and speed-distance word problems
  • Series, blood relations, syllogisms, and seating arrangement puzzles
  • Reading comprehension, error spotting, and sentence correction
  • Output prediction and basic logic in C/Java/Python snippets
  • Simple algorithmic coding on strings, arrays, or number manipulation
How to prepare
  • Drill quantitative and logical sets on IndiaBix and PrepInsta free sections
  • Time yourself per section to build the pacing sectional cutoffs demand
  • Revise output-based MCQs for loops, arrays, and pointers
  • Solve 15–20 easy coding problems on HackerRank to warm up syntax
Common mistakes
  • Over-investing in one section and missing another section's cutoff
  • Leaving blanks when there is no negative marking
  • Ignoring the verbal section as an easy afterthought
What they look for
  • Balanced scores clearing every sectional minimum
  • Working code that compiles and passes visible cases
  • Steady pace rather than rushing the last section
2

Technical Interview

~30–45 min
CS fundamentals and project depth

A conversational round covering data structures, OOP, DBMS/SQL, and one or two projects from your resume. Expect to explain your code choices and answer follow-ups on why. Depth is moderate; interviewers reward clarity and honesty over memorized definitions.

Example questions
  • Core data structures: arrays, linked lists, stacks, trees and their uses
  • OOP pillars with a concrete real-world example each
  • SQL joins, normalization, keys, and simple query writing
  • End-to-end walkthrough of a resume project and your specific role
  • One easy-to-medium DSA problem explained aloud
How to prepare
  • Prepare a two-minute and a five-minute version of your best project
  • Revise OOP and DBMS from GeeksforGeeks fundamentals articles
  • Practice writing basic SQL queries by hand on paper
  • Rehearse explaining one sorting or searching algorithm step by step
Common mistakes
  • Listing project tech you cannot actually explain
  • Reciting textbook definitions without an example
  • Going silent instead of thinking out loud on a problem
What they look for
  • Clear ownership and honest scoping of your project work
  • Correct fundamentals explained in your own words
  • Structured problem-solving even when the answer is imperfect
3

HR Interview

~15–30 min
Fit, communication, and stability

A behavioral round on your background, motivation, relocation and shift flexibility, and basic company awareness. It is largely a confirmation round but confidence and clear communication still decide borderline cases.

Example questions
  • Why NTT Data and what you know about the company
  • Willingness to relocate and work in shifts
  • Strengths, weaknesses, and a short self-introduction
  • A situational teamwork or conflict story
  • Career goals and long-term intent
How to prepare
  • Read NTT Data's business lines and recent India news for context
  • Prepare one honest STAR story about teamwork or a setback
  • Rehearse a crisp 45-second self-introduction
  • Decide your genuine stance on relocation and shifts in advance
Common mistakes
  • Showing hesitation about relocation or shifts
  • A rambling, unstructured self-introduction
  • Zero knowledge of what the company actually does
What they look for
  • Confident, concise communication
  • Genuine flexibility on location and role
  • Authentic, specific answers over rehearsed clichés

What to master

  • Quantitative aptitude
  • Logical reasoning
  • Verbal ability
  • Data structures
  • OOP concepts
  • DBMS and SQL
  • C/Java/Python basics
  • Project explanation

Eligibility

Typically B.E./B.Tech (CSE/IT/ECE and allied) with ~60% throughout and no active backlogs; exact rules vary by drive.

Your prep plan

  • Days 1–4: rebuild quantitative and logical reasoning speed with daily timed sets
  • Days 5–7: revise C/Java/Python output MCQs and basic coding
  • Days 8–10: consolidate DSA basics and 20 easy problems
  • Days 11–12: revise OOP, DBMS, and hand-written SQL
  • Day 13: prepare two project pitches and STAR stories
  • Day 14: full mock assessment plus HR question rehearsal
  • Do two timed aptitude sections and 10 output MCQs
  • Revise OOP, SQL joins, and one project end to end
  • Prepare self-intro, relocation stance, and one teamwork story

Frequently asked questions

Is there negative marking in the online test?

Reports indicate no negative marking, so attempt every question, but sectional cutoffs mean you must clear each section, not just the total.

How hard is the coding section?

Basic to moderate: string, array, and number-manipulation problems plus output-prediction MCQs. Strong DSA is a bonus, not a requirement.

Do all drives have a group discussion?

No. Some campus drives include a GD between the test and interview, but many off-campus flows skip it. Confirm from your drive notice.
