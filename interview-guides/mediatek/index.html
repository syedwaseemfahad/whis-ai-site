MediaTek Interview Guide (2026)

MediaTek designs SoCs for smartphones, connectivity, smart TVs, and more, hiring heavily for RTL design, verification, physical design, DFT, and embedded/system software. Interviews are digital-design and fundamentals heavy for hardware and C/DSA heavy for software. MediaTek is known for strong fresher and early-career packages in India, so the technical bar for new grads is high.

Round-by-round: exactly what's asked & how to prepare

1

Written / Online Test

~60–90 min
Digital design, aptitude, and domain MCQs

A timed test heavy on digital logic and fundamentals for hardware tracks, or C output plus DSA for software tracks, alongside aptitude. Depth in your domain and accuracy under time pressure decide the shortlist.

Example questions
  • Combinational/sequential logic and K-maps
  • Flip-flops, counters, and FSM behavior
  • Number systems and Boolean simplification
  • C output and pointer questions (software)
  • Quantitative and logical aptitude
How to prepare
  • Drill digital logic from Morris Mano
  • Practice K-maps, counters, and FSM timing
  • For SW, revise C output and medium DSA
  • Do timed aptitude sets on IndiaBIX (free)
Common mistakes
  • Weak on sequential-circuit timing
  • Careless K-map and Boolean errors
  • Ignoring aptitude weightage
What they look for
  • Strong digital fundamentals
  • Accuracy under time pressure
  • Balanced section performance
2

Technical Round 1 (domain fundamentals)

~45–60 min
Digital/RTL depth or C + DSA

For hardware: RTL, FSM design, timing, and Verilog/SystemVerilog with a coding-on-paper feel. For software: embedded C, pointers, bit manipulation, and a DSA problem. Interviewers push into edge cases and design justification.

Example questions
  • FSM design (Mealy vs Moore) and encoding
  • Setup/hold, clock domain, and metastability
  • Verilog constructs and synthesizable code
  • Bit manipulation and pointer questions (SW)
  • DSA on arrays, linked lists, and trees (SW)
How to prepare
  • Write small Verilog modules (FSM, counter, FIFO)
  • Review timing, CDC, and metastability basics
  • For SW, drill embedded C and NeetCode patterns
  • Practice explaining design choices aloud
Common mistakes
  • Non-synthesizable or sloppy Verilog
  • Ignoring clock-domain and timing concerns
  • Pointer/bit-manipulation slips (software)
What they look for
  • Clean, synthesizable design thinking
  • Timing and CDC awareness
  • Solid low-level coding
3

Technical Round 2 + Hiring Manager

~45–60 min
Project deep-dive and applied design

Detailed project questioning plus applied design/debug problems, often with the manager present. Expect follow-ups on verification methodology, coverage, or system-level trade-offs for hardware, and design/debugging for software. Fit and communication are assessed too.

Example questions
  • Defending RTL/verification decisions in projects
  • Coverage, assertions, and testbench thinking
  • Applied 'design a block/module for X' prompts
  • System and power/area/timing trade-offs
  • Debugging a described failure to root cause
How to prepare
  • Prepare 2 projects defensible at deep technical level
  • Review verification concepts (coverage, assertions, UVM basics)
  • Practice applied design/debug prompts
  • Read MediaTek's product/SoC areas relevant to you
Common mistakes
  • Vague project ownership
  • No grasp of verification methodology
  • Generic 'why MediaTek' answer
What they look for
  • Design and verification maturity
  • Ownership under follow-ups
  • Clear domain motivation

What to master

  • Digital design & VLSI
  • FSMs, timing, CDC, metastability
  • Verilog/SystemVerilog & verification
  • Embedded C & bit manipulation
  • Data structures & algorithms
  • Computer architecture basics
  • Number systems & Boolean algebra
  • Project design & debugging

Eligibility

Role-based: BE/BTech/ME/MTech in ECE/EE for VLSI/RTL/verification; CS/ECE for embedded software. MTech/MS in VLSI or a strong design portfolio helps notably for hardware roles.

Your prep plan

  • Days 10–12: Turn 2 projects into deep, defensible narratives
  • Days 13–14: Mock interviews plus aptitude speed practice
  • Speed-review FSMs, timing, and CDC (or C + DSA for software)
  • Re-write 2–3 small Verilog modules from scratch
  • Rehearse project deep-dives with verification and trade-off details

Frequently asked questions

Is MediaTek mostly a hardware company for interviews?

Hardware/VLSI is the largest track, and those interviews are digital-design and verification heavy. But MediaTek also hires embedded and system software engineers who face C and DSA rounds.

How good are MediaTek's fresher packages in India?

Strong. MediaTek is known among Tier-1 product companies for competitive early-career pay, which is why the new-grad technical bar is high.

Do I need UVM knowledge?

For verification roles, familiarity with coverage, assertions, and UVM basics helps a lot. For design roles, solid RTL, timing, and Verilog matter more.
