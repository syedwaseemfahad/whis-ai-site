LinkedIn SWE Interview Guide (2026)

LinkedIn hires against a clear leveling rubric and evaluates each round on explicit signals rather than trick puzzles. Coding rounds favor clean, working, well-tested code over exotic tricks, and system design probes real feed/graph/messaging-scale tradeoffs. The behavioral round is not a formality: interviewers look for alignment with LinkedIn's culture (members first, relationships matter, act like an owner).

Round-by-round: exactly what's asked & how to prepare

1

Recruiter Screen

~30 min
Fit, level, motivation

A recruiter walks through your background, target team, level expectations, and logistics. They may ask a couple of high-level technical or resume-depth questions to calibrate. This round sets which coding/design tracks you get and is your chance to learn the exact loop shape.

Example questions
  • Why LinkedIn and why this team
  • Recent project you owned end to end
  • Level calibration against scope of past work
  • Compensation and timeline expectations
How to prepare
  • Draft a 90-second story per resume project with impact metrics
  • Read the LinkedIn Engineering Blog for one team-relevant post
  • Prepare questions about team charter and on-call reality
  • Free: LinkedIn's own careers/engineering pages for culture language
Common mistakes
  • Being vague about your specific contribution vs the team's
  • Not knowing which product area you're interviewing for
What they look for
  • Clear, quantified ownership stories
  • Realistic self-assessment of level
2

Technical Phone Screen

~45–60 min
Coding correctness and communication

A live coding session in CoderPad, usually one or two medium problems on arrays, strings, hash maps, trees, or graphs. The interviewer cares about how you clarify, reason aloud, handle edge cases, and test. Optimal complexity plus running/tested code is expected within the time box.

Example questions
  • Hash-map and two-pointer array manipulation
  • Tree/graph traversal (BFS/DFS) with a real-world framing
  • Interval merging or scheduling variants
  • String parsing with careful edge-case handling
How to prepare
  • Grind ~60 mediums on NeetCode 150 (free) focusing on graphs/trees
  • Practice narrating clarify → approach → code → test out loud
  • Rehearse in a plain editor (CoderPad has no autocomplete)
  • Free: LeetCode free tier + Tech Interview Handbook
Common mistakes
  • Jumping to code before confirming input assumptions
  • Leaving code untested or ignoring edge cases
What they look for
  • Correct, compiling, complexity-optimal solution
  • Proactive edge-case and test discussion
3

Onsite Coding (x2)

~45–60 min each
Algorithms under pressure with clean code

Two independent coding rounds, each with medium-to-hard problems. Interviewers push for the optimal approach, then may add a follow-up twist or ask you to refactor for readability. Code quality, naming, and modularity are graded, not just passing tests.

Example questions
  • Graph shortest-path or connectivity on a network/graph theme
  • Dynamic programming on sequences or grids
  • Heap/priority-queue for top-K or streaming problems
  • Design of a small in-memory data structure with clean API
How to prepare
  • Do timed 45-min mocks with a peer or Pramp (free)
  • Build a template habit for DP, graphs, and heaps
  • Practice writing production-quality helper functions
  • Free: NeetCode roadmap videos for pattern intuition
Common mistakes
  • Over-optimizing prematurely and running out of time
  • Writing dense, unreadable one-liners under pressure
What they look for
  • Pattern recognition and quick optimal framing
  • Readable, well-decomposed code
4

System Design

~45–60 min
Scalable distributed design (senior+ mainly)

Given an open-ended prompt often mirroring LinkedIn scale (feed ranking, connection graph, messaging, notifications). You drive requirements, capacity estimates, API and data model, then deep-dive on storage, caching, and consistency tradeoffs. New grads may instead get a lighter design or OOD round.

Example questions
  • News-feed generation and fan-out on write vs read
  • Social graph storage and friend-of-friend queries at scale
  • Real-time messaging with delivery and read-receipt guarantees
  • Rate limiting or a notification delivery pipeline
How to prepare
  • Study fan-out, sharding, caching, and CAP tradeoffs
  • Practice a repeatable framework: requirements → estimate → API → data → scale
  • Do 2–3 mock designs out loud with a whiteboard tool
  • Free: Grokking-style notes via github.com/donnemartin/system-design-primer
Common mistakes
  • Diving into components before pinning down requirements
  • Ignoring failure modes, bottlenecks, and back-of-envelope numbers
What they look for
  • Structured tradeoff reasoning
  • Comfort quantifying load and choosing storage sensibly
5

Behavioral / Values

~45 min
Culture fit and collaboration

A hiring manager or senior engineer explores conflict, ownership, ambiguity, and impact through past examples. LinkedIn maps answers to its stated values, so grounding stories in members-first thinking and collaboration lands well. Expect probing follow-ups on your specific role in outcomes.

Example questions
  • Handling disagreement with a teammate or manager
  • Owning a project through ambiguity or a missed deadline
  • Mentoring or unblocking others
  • A decision where you prioritized long-term member impact
How to prepare
  • Prepare 6–8 STAR stories mapped to LinkedIn's values
  • Quantify impact in each story where possible
  • Practice concise 2-minute delivery per story
  • Free: LinkedIn's published culture/values pages
Common mistakes
  • Generic answers with no measurable outcome
  • Blaming others instead of showing ownership
What they look for
  • Authentic, specific, self-aware stories
  • Values alignment without sounding rehearsed

What to master

  • Arrays, strings, hash maps
  • Trees and graph traversal
  • Dynamic programming
  • Heaps and priority queues
  • Distributed system design (feed, graph, messaging)
  • Caching and sharding
  • Concurrency basics
  • Behavioral / values fit

Eligibility

open — role-based (new grad through staff; level sets loop shape)

How the package is structured

  • Comp = base + ~10% bonus + 4-yr Microsoft/LinkedIn RSUs plus annual refreshers.
  • Among the most generous non-FAANG-tier payers, especially at Senior/Staff.
  • US pays ~3–4x India for the same level; India (Bangalore) org is strong.

Your prep plan

  • Complete NeetCode 150 with emphasis on graphs, trees, and DP
  • Do 4+ timed 45-min coding mocks on Pramp or with peers
  • Build and rehearse one system-design framework end to end
  • Read 2–3 LinkedIn Engineering Blog posts for scale context
  • Write 6–8 STAR stories mapped to LinkedIn values
  • Practice coding in a bare editor to mimic CoderPad
  • Re-drill your weakest pattern (usually graphs or DP)
  • Rehearse top 4 STAR stories aloud, 2 min each
  • Skim your system-design framework and one deep-dive topic

Frequently asked questions

Is LinkedIn's loop different now that it's under Microsoft?

Yes. LinkedIn still runs its own recruiting, rubric, and CoderPad-based loop distinct from Microsoft's standard process, though comp bands are aligned. Prepare for LinkedIn's specifics.

Do new grads get a system design round?

Usually not a full distributed-design round; new grads more often get an extra coding or object-oriented design round instead. Senior and above get real system design.

How much does the behavioral round matter?

A lot. A weak values round can sink an otherwise strong candidate because LinkedIn weights culture fit explicitly in its rubric.
