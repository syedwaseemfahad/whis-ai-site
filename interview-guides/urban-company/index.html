Urban Company Interview Guide (2026)

Urban Company runs a two-sided marketplace with real scheduling, matching, and payments complexity, so its loop weighs machine coding and design heavily alongside DSA. The machine-coding round can run up to three hours and expects production-quality, extensible code. Interviewers reward pragmatic engineers who model real marketplace problems cleanly.

Round-by-round: exactly what's asked & how to prepare

1

Online Coding Test

~90 min
Algorithmic screening

Around three problems of medium-to-hard difficulty within roughly 90 minutes. Focus areas repeat on graphs and DP. Correct, complete submissions matter more than attempting everything.

Example questions
  • Connected components on a service-area graph
  • DP for optimal slot/booking selection
  • Greedy assignment of pros to jobs
  • String or matrix simulation
  • Two-pointer / sliding-window on schedules
How to prepare
  • Grind graph and DP mediums (leetcode.com)
  • Work Striver's A2Z sheet with timing (takeuforward.org)
  • Practice union-find and BFS/DFS templates
  • Do 2–3 timed 3-problem sets
Common mistakes
  • Spreading time too thin across problems
  • Weak union-find or component-counting reflexes
  • Skipping edge cases on grid problems
What they look for
  • Fully correct submissions on the graph/DP items
  • Optimal complexity
  • Clean, tested code
2

Technical – DSA

~60 min
Live algorithmic reasoning

One or two medium-hard problems, commonly graphs and DP, with follow-ups. You should talk through the approach and complexity before coding. Clean, correct code under time pressure is the goal.

Example questions
  • Graph connectivity and reachability
  • DP on partitioning or scheduling
  • Heap-based top-K or nearest matching
  • Interval merging for availability windows
  • Matrix traversal / flood fill
How to prepare
  • Solve 15–20 graph and DP mediums (neetcode.io)
  • Narrate brute-force-to-optimal in every practice
  • Drill complexity analysis
  • Run peer mock interviews
Common mistakes
  • Coding before aligning on the approach
  • Going quiet mid-problem
  • Forgetting to analyze complexity
What they look for
  • Structured approach and clear trade-offs
  • Correct edge-case handling
  • Accurate complexity analysis
3

Machine Coding / LLD

~2–3 hrs
Production-quality object design

A long-form build where you design and implement a working system with clean classes, extensibility, and tests. Urban Company weights this heavily and expects code that could plausibly ship. Requirements often expand mid-round to test extensibility.

Example questions
  • Design a task manager using OOP and set structures (LRU-cache-like)
  • Booking/slot allocation engine
  • Splitwise-style expense splitter
  • In-memory marketplace matching service
  • Rating and review aggregation module
How to prepare
  • Practice full machine-coding builds under 2–3 hours
  • Master SOLID and common patterns (refactoring.guru)
  • Design interfaces first, keep classes small
  • Study the low-level-design-primer (github.com/prasadgujar)
Common mistakes
  • Non-compiling or untested code at the end
  • God classes and tight coupling
  • No room to absorb the extension follow-up
What they look for
  • Working, extensible, readable code
  • Clear separation of concerns
  • Graceful handling of the added requirement
4

System Design (HLD)

~45–60 min
Marketplace design at scale

An open design centered on Urban Company's domain — matching, scheduling, or payments — where you drive requirements, APIs, and data model. Expect discussion of consistency, availability, and how the two-sided marketplace scales. Stronger emphasis from SDE-2 up.

Example questions
  • Design a driverless-cab or pro-booking system
  • Real-time pro-to-job matching
  • Scheduling with double-booking prevention
  • Payments and payouts with idempotency
  • Search for available pros by location and skill
How to prepare
  • Study the System Design Primer (github.com/donnemartin)
  • Watch marketplace-design breakdowns (Gaurav Sen, ByteByteGo)
  • Practice QPS and storage estimation
  • Design 4 systems end to end with a timer
Common mistakes
  • Skipping requirement clarification
  • Ignoring booking-consistency / race conditions
  • No data model or scale reasoning
What they look for
  • Clear scoping and back-of-envelope math
  • Sound consistency choices for bookings
  • Awareness of bottlenecks and trade-offs
5

Projects & Culture Fit

~30–45 min
Depth, ownership, and values

A deep dive into your projects and a culture conversation, often with senior tech leadership. They probe technical ownership, decision-making, and alignment with a fast-moving, quality-obsessed culture. Motivation for the marketplace space comes up.

Example questions
  • A system you built and owned end to end
  • A hard technical trade-off you made
  • Handling ambiguity or shifting scope
  • A conflict and how you resolved it
  • Why Urban Company / marketplaces
How to prepare
  • Prepare 5–6 metric-backed STAR stories
  • Be able to whiteboard your own project's design
  • Research Urban Company's model and challenges
  • Prepare thoughtful leadership-level questions
Common mistakes
  • Vague, unquantified project claims
  • Poor conflict framing
  • No specific interest in the company
What they look for
  • Strong technical ownership
  • Clear, measured decision reasoning
  • Genuine culture and mission fit

What to master

  • Graphs & union-find
  • Dynamic programming
  • Heaps & intervals
  • Low-level / machine-coding design
  • OOP & SOLID
  • Marketplace system design
  • Concurrency & consistency
  • Databases & caching

How the package is structured

  • Structure is fixed base + variable + joining bonus + ESOP; ESOP scales with level.
  • Cash base is competitive post-IPO era; negotiate fixed with leverage from competing offers.
  • Ask about ESOP vesting, strike, and liquidity now that the company is public.
  • Level placement drives comp far more than round-by-round negotiation.

Indicative 2026 market ranges aggregated from public sources (levels.fyi, Glassdoor, AmbitionBox, candidate reports). Compensation varies widely by location, team, level calibration, and negotiation — use these as directional benchmarks, not guarantees.

Your prep plan

  • Days 1–4: graphs, union-find, and DP to solid medium level
  • Days 5–6: intervals, heaps, and a timed OA run
  • Days 7–9: two full machine-coding builds under time pressure
  • Days 10–11: two marketplace HLD designs with numbers
  • Day 12: six STAR stories plus a project design walkthrough
  • Days 13–14: mixed mocks and weak-area review
  • Rebuild one machine-coding problem end to end for warmth
  • Review a marketplace HLD template with booking consistency
  • Lock in STAR stories and your 'why Urban Company' answer

Frequently asked questions

How long is the machine-coding round really?

It can run 2–3 hours and expects working, extensible, tested code — practice full builds, not just class sketches.

Which matters more, DSA or design?

Both gate you, but Urban Company weights machine coding and design more than many peers, so don't under-invest there.

Do freshers face the full 3-hour build?

SDE-1 machine coding is usually shorter and simpler; the long-form build is more common at SDE-2.
