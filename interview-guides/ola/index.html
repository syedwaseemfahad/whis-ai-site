Ola Interview Guide (2026)

Ola's engineering spans ride matching, maps/ETA, payments, and the Ola Electric stack, so interviews probe both raw problem-solving and how you reason about latency-sensitive, high-throughput systems. Expect medium-hard DSA early and increasingly system-focused conversations as you go up levels. Interviewers value candidates who connect code to real product constraints like surge pricing or driver allocation.

Round-by-round: exactly what's asked & how to prepare

1

Online Assessment

~90 min
Timed algorithmic coding

A HackerEarth/HackerRank-style test with 2–3 problems ranging from easy to medium-hard. You are scored on correctness against hidden tests and, for some problems, on passing large inputs within the time limit. Partial credit exists, so bank the easy problem first.

Example questions
  • Sliding-window over a stream of ride events
  • Shortest path / Dijkstra variant on a road graph
  • DP on interval scheduling for driver shifts
  • Greedy allocation with a priority queue
  • String parsing of trip logs
How to prepare
  • Grind LeetCode arrays/strings/graphs/DP mediums (leetcode.com)
  • Time yourself on Striver's A2Z DSA sheet (takeuforward.org)
  • Practice fast I/O and edge cases on HackerEarth mock tests
  • Rehearse writing bug-free code without a debugger
Common mistakes
  • Chasing the hardest problem and leaving easy points on the table
  • Ignoring integer overflow and empty-input edge cases
  • Not testing against large inputs before submitting
What they look for
  • All-tests-pass on at least the two easier problems
  • Clean, readable solutions with correct complexity
  • Efficient use of the time budget
2

Technical – DSA

~45–60 min
Live problem-solving depth

One to two medium-hard problems on a shared editor, usually graphs, trees, or DP. The interviewer cares as much about how you narrate trade-offs as the final code. Expect follow-ups that tighten complexity or change the input scale.

Example questions
  • Graph connectivity for road-network partitioning
  • K-nearest drivers via heap or grid bucketing
  • DP for optimal fare/route segmentation
  • LRU-style cache for hot map tiles
  • Interval merging for surge windows
How to prepare
  • Solve 15–20 graph and DP mediums, dry-running each aloud
  • Practice stating brute force → optimized before coding (NeetCode roadmap)
  • Rehearse complexity analysis for every solution
  • Do 3–4 mock interviews on Pramp
Common mistakes
  • Coding before agreeing on the approach
  • Going silent while thinking
  • Skipping complexity discussion the interviewer expects
What they look for
  • Structured brute-force-to-optimal progression
  • Correct handling of tricky edge cases
  • Clear complexity reasoning
3

Machine Coding / LLD

~60–90 min
Object modelling and clean code

You design and implement a small system with real classes, extensibility, and thread-safety considerations. Interviewers reward clean abstractions and testable code over cramming every feature. Common at SDE-2 and above.

Example questions
  • Design an in-memory ride-booking allocator
  • Task manager / scheduler backed by a set data structure
  • Rate limiter for the pricing service
  • Parking-lot or cab-fleet OOP model
  • Notification dispatcher with pluggable channels
How to prepare
  • Study LLD patterns (refactoring.guru for SOLID and design patterns)
  • Build 3–4 machine-coding problems end to end in your language
  • Practice writing interfaces first, then implementations
  • Read the low-level-design-primer (github.com/prasadgujar)
Common mistakes
  • Jumping to code without clarifying requirements
  • God classes with no separation of concerns
  • Ignoring concurrency where the prompt implies it
What they look for
  • Clean, extensible class boundaries
  • Working, compilable code within the time box
  • Sensible handling of edge cases and thread-safety
4

HLD / System Design

~45–60 min
Designing at ride-hailing scale

An open-ended design of a system Ola actually runs, focused on scale, latency, and consistency trade-offs. You drive the whiteboard: requirements, APIs, data model, bottlenecks. Depth expectations rise sharply from SDE-2 to SDE-3.

Example questions
  • Design real-time driver-rider matching
  • Surge pricing pipeline with fresh demand signals
  • Live location tracking and ETA at millions of pings/sec
  • Payments and wallet with idempotency
  • Geo-sharded search for nearby drivers
How to prepare
  • Work through the System Design Primer (github.com/donnemartin)
  • Watch Gaurav Sen and ByteByteGo design breakdowns (YouTube)
  • Practice estimating QPS, storage, and bandwidth aloud
  • Design 4–5 systems end to end with a timer
Common mistakes
  • Skipping requirement and scale clarification
  • Hand-waving the data model and consistency choices
  • No discussion of bottlenecks or failure modes
What they look for
  • Clear requirement scoping and back-of-envelope numbers
  • Justified storage and consistency trade-offs
  • Awareness of hot-partition and latency pitfalls
5

Hiring Manager / HR

~20–40 min
Ownership and culture fit

A mix of past-project deep dive and behavioral questions about ownership, ambiguity, and speed. Ola runs lean and fast, so they probe for bias-to-action and end-to-end responsibility. Expect a few questions on why Ola specifically.

Example questions
  • A project you shipped under a tight deadline
  • A production incident you owned end to end
  • Disagreement with a teammate or manager
  • Handling shifting requirements
  • Why mobility / why Ola
How to prepare
  • Prepare 5–6 STAR stories with metrics
  • Research Ola's product lines and recent launches
  • Prepare thoughtful questions about the team
  • Practice concise 2-minute story delivery
Common mistakes
  • Vague stories with no measurable impact
  • Blaming teammates in conflict examples
  • No specific reason for wanting Ola
What they look for
  • Concrete ownership with quantified outcomes
  • Calm reasoning through ambiguity
  • Genuine interest in the mission

What to master

  • Arrays & strings
  • Graphs & shortest paths
  • Dynamic programming
  • Trees & heaps
  • Low-level design / OOP
  • Distributed system design
  • Concurrency basics
  • Databases & caching

How the package is structured

  • Structure is fixed base + performance variable + ESOP; ESOP weight rises steeply from SDE-3 up.
  • Competing offers move the fixed base the most; negotiate that first.
  • Ask about ESOP strike price, vesting, and the latest 409A/valuation to value equity honestly.
  • Level placement drives comp more than negotiation — aim to interview at the highest justifiable level.

Indicative 2026 market ranges aggregated from public sources (levels.fyi, Glassdoor, AmbitionBox, candidate reports). Compensation varies widely by location, team, level calibration, and negotiation — use these as directional benchmarks, not guarantees.

Your prep plan

  • Days 1–4: drill arrays, strings, and graphs to solid medium level
  • Days 5–7: DP and trees plus one timed OA simulation
  • Days 8–9: build two machine-coding problems end to end
  • Days 10–11: practice three HLD designs with QPS math
  • Day 12: write and rehearse six STAR stories
  • Days 13–14: mixed mocks and review weak topics
  • Re-solve your strongest graph and DP patterns for warmth
  • Skim one LLD and one HLD design template end to end
  • Lock in three STAR stories and your 'why Ola' answer

Frequently asked questions

Is Ola Cabs the same interview as Ola Electric?

The core DSA and design bar is similar, but Ola Electric roles may lean harder into embedded, IoT, or platform topics depending on the team. Confirm the team's focus with your recruiter.

How much system design should a fresher expect?

SDE-1 loops are DSA-heavy with light LLD. Meaningful HLD kicks in from SDE-2 onward.

Does the OA allow partial scoring?

Yes, most Ola OAs award per-test-case credit, so submit working partial solutions rather than nothing.
