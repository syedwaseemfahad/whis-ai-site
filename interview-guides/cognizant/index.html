Cognizant Interview Guide (2026)

Cognizant hires freshers through its GenC family of programs, with a communication assessment, an aptitude-plus-technical assessment, and technical and HR interviews. What is distinctive is the split between GenC (for candidates with basic programming) and GenC Next/GenC Pro (for strong coders with full-stack ability), where the advanced track includes a paid internship and a differential, higher package.

Round-by-round: exactly what's asked & how to prepare

1

Communication + Aptitude Assessment

~60-90 min
English, aptitude, and reasoning

Combines a communication/English section with quantitative aptitude, logical reasoning, and verbal ability. Acts as the first elimination filter with sectional expectations.

Example questions
  • Quantitative aptitude: arithmetic and DI
  • Logical reasoning: series, puzzles, arrangements
  • Verbal ability and comprehension
  • English usage and vocabulary
  • Basic sentence construction/grammar
How to prepare
  • Take timed aptitude mocks on free platforms
  • Revise grammar and vocabulary
  • Practice DI and reasoning daily
  • Do full communication-plus-aptitude mocks
Common mistakes
  • Neglecting the communication/English portion
  • Poor time budgeting
  • Weak reasoning under time pressure
What they look for
  • Balanced accuracy across sections
  • Good English usage
  • Speed and reasoning strength
2

Technical Assessment

~45-60 min
Programming fundamentals and coding

Technical MCQs and, for engineering/advanced clusters, coding. Depending on the skill cluster you may see Java, SQL, Python, C#, web basics, or cloud fundamentals. GenC Next expects genuine coding and full-stack awareness.

Example questions
  • Programming MCQs in Java/Python/C#
  • SQL query and DBMS basics
  • Web fundamentals (HTML/CSS/JS basics for relevant clusters)
  • A coding problem on arrays/strings (advanced track)
  • OOP and CS fundamentals MCQs
How to prepare
  • Revise the language/cluster relevant to the role
  • Practice SQL queries and DBMS basics
  • For GenC Next, solve easy-medium DSA problems
  • Do technical MCQ mocks
Common mistakes
  • Preparing the wrong cluster's tech stack
  • Weak SQL fundamentals
  • Under-preparing coding for the advanced track
What they look for
  • Solid fundamentals in the relevant stack
  • Working coding logic (advanced track)
  • SQL competence
3

Technical Interview

20-40 min
Fundamentals, projects, and problem-solving

A resume-driven technical discussion covering your language, projects, and CS basics, with more coding depth for GenC Next candidates.

Example questions
  • Project walkthrough and your contribution
  • OOP concepts with examples
  • SQL/DBMS questions
  • A small logic or coding problem
  • Data structures you have used
How to prepare
  • Prepare crisp project explanations
  • Revise OOP, DBMS, and one language
  • Practice explaining code aloud
  • Do a mock technical interview with follow-ups
Common mistakes
  • Overstating resume skills
  • Weak project ownership
  • Freezing on a simple logic question
What they look for
  • Genuine project depth
  • Solid fundamentals
  • Clear problem-solving
4

HR Interview

10-20 min
Communication, culture fit, and logistics

Evaluates soft skills, communication, cultural alignment, motivations, and joining logistics including relocation and the service agreement.

Example questions
  • Tell me about yourself and your background
  • Why Cognizant and this role
  • Relocation and shift flexibility
  • Strengths, weaknesses, and goals
  • Comfort with the internship/bond terms (advanced track)
How to prepare
  • Prepare a concise self-introduction
  • Research Cognizant's business and culture
  • Have consistent flexibility answers
  • Keep documents ready
Common mistakes
  • Generic, unresearched answers
  • Inconsistency across rounds
  • Hesitation on standard terms
What they look for
  • Clear communication
  • Cultural fit and motivation
  • Flexibility and readiness

What to master

  • Quantitative aptitude and DI
  • Logical reasoning
  • Verbal ability and English usage
  • One language relevant to your cluster (Java/Python/C#)
  • SQL and DBMS
  • Web fundamentals (for relevant roles)
  • Core DSA (advanced track)
  • Resume project depth

Eligibility

More than 60% in 10th and 12th and minimum 60% in graduation; qualifications typically BE/B.Tech/ME/M.Tech/MCA; maximum gap of about 1 year after Class 12 or between semesters; no active backlogs. Exact criteria vary by drive.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between GenC and GenC Next?

GenC is for candidates with basic programming ability; GenC Next is for strong coders with full-stack skills, includes a paid internship, and offers a higher, differential package.

Which tech stack should I prepare?

It depends on your assigned skill cluster. Confirm whether it is Java, Python, C#, web, or cloud focused and prepare accordingly.

How important is communication?

Very. There is a dedicated communication assessment and the HR round weighs soft skills, since roles are often client-facing.

Is coding mandatory for GenC?

Programming knowledge is recommended and increasingly expected, though the base GenC track is lighter on coding than GenC Next.

Do backlogs and gaps matter?

You typically need no active backlogs and a limited education gap (often up to about a year). Confirm the exact rule per drive.

Is there an internship before joining?

The advanced GenC Next track commonly includes a mandatory paid internship period before the differential salary applies.
