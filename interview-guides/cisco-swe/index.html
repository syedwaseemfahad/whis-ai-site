Cisco SWE Interview Guide (2026)

Cisco builds networking, security, and infrastructure software, so its loop pairs standard data-structure coding with fundamentals in operating systems, networking (TCP/IP, protocols), and sometimes low-level or concurrency topics. The algorithm bar is moderate; what distinguishes strong candidates is solid CS fundamentals and, for many teams, genuine networking or systems knowledge plus good collaboration.

Round-by-round: exactly what's asked & how to prepare

1

Online Assessment / Recruiter Screen

~60–90 min
Baseline coding and fundamentals

For many pipelines (especially early-career) a timed OA with coding problems and sometimes MCQs on CS fundamentals. Experienced candidates may get a recruiter conversation instead. The OA covers medium DSA and may include OS/networking multiple-choice.

Example questions
  • Array and string problems
  • Hash-map and set usage
  • Basic recursion and traversal
  • OS/networking fundamentals MCQs
How to prepare
  • Do timed medium problems on LeetCode
  • Review CS fundamentals: OS scheduling, memory, TCP/IP basics
  • Practice recursion and traversal patterns
  • Read the role description to gauge how networking-heavy it is
Common mistakes
  • Neglecting fundamentals MCQs and focusing only on coding
  • Poor time management on the OA
What they look for
  • Solid coding baseline
  • Fundamentals knowledge
2

Technical Phone Screen

~45–60 min
DSA plus CS fundamentals

A live coding problem of medium difficulty, often followed by conceptual questions on data structures, OS, or networking depending on the team. Interviewers want correct code plus the ability to reason about how it maps to real systems.

Example questions
  • Linked lists and pointer manipulation
  • Graph or tree traversal
  • String and array processing
  • Concept follow-ups on OS/networking
How to prepare
  • Solve 40 medium problems across arrays, strings, linked lists, trees, and graphs
  • Review OS concepts: processes/threads, locks, memory management
  • Refresh networking basics: TCP vs UDP, handshake, routing/switching
  • Practice explaining code and its complexity clearly
Common mistakes
  • Strong code but weak on fundamentals follow-ups
  • Ignoring edge cases in pointer-heavy problems
What they look for
  • Correct code with clear reasoning
  • Fundamentals depth
3

Systems / Networking Deep Dive

~45–60 min
Applied systems and networking knowledge

A round probing operating systems, networking, and sometimes concurrency or low-level programming, tailored to the team (routing, security, collaboration software). May include a practical coding task involving sockets, threads, or protocol handling. Depth expected scales with seniority.

Example questions
  • TCP/IP, sockets, and protocol behavior
  • Concurrency: threads, locks, race conditions
  • Memory management and pointers (C/C++ teams)
  • Debugging a networking or systems scenario
  • Reliability and error handling in distributed nodes
How to prepare
  • Review the TCP/IP stack, handshake, congestion control, and common protocols
  • Practice concurrency problems and reason about race conditions/deadlock
  • For C/C++ roles, refresh pointers, memory, and undefined behavior
  • Work an example involving sockets or a simple protocol
Common mistakes
  • Shallow networking knowledge for a networking company
  • Ignoring thread-safety in concurrency answers
What they look for
  • Genuine systems/networking depth
  • Careful concurrency reasoning
4

System / Component Design

~45–60 min
Designing reliable infrastructure software

For mid/senior roles, a design round scoped to infrastructure: a monitoring/telemetry service, a message-routing component, a config-distribution system, or a scalable API. Focus on reliability, fault tolerance, and clear component boundaries rather than consumer-scale distributed diagrams.

Example questions
  • Telemetry / monitoring pipeline
  • Message routing and delivery guarantees
  • Configuration distribution to many nodes
  • Fault tolerance and failover
  • API and service boundaries
How to prepare
  • Practice designing a monitoring or telemetry pipeline
  • Review delivery guarantees (at-least-once, exactly-once) and queues
  • Study System Design Primer on GitHub for core building blocks
  • Do a mock focused on reliability and fault tolerance
Common mistakes
  • Ignoring failure modes and recovery
  • Overcomplicating instead of a clean, reliable design
What they look for
  • Reliability-first design
  • Clear component boundaries
5

Behavioral / Hiring Manager

~30–45 min
Collaboration, ownership, and inclusion

A conversation about teamwork, past projects, handling conflict, and alignment with Cisco's people-focused, inclusive culture. Expect standard behavioral questions plus discussion of how you collaborate across teams and handle setbacks.

Example questions
  • A project you drove and its impact
  • Working through a team conflict
  • Handling a failure or setback
  • Collaborating across functions or geographies
How to prepare
  • Prepare 5 STAR stories with clear contribution and outcomes
  • Have a genuine reason for Cisco and its domain
  • Rehearse a conflict-resolution and a failure/learning story
  • Prepare questions about the team and its systems
Common mistakes
  • Generic stories with no measurable impact
  • No specific interest in Cisco's mission
What they look for
  • Collaborative ownership
  • Authentic culture fit

What to master

  • Arrays, strings, linked lists
  • Trees and graphs
  • Operating systems fundamentals
  • Networking (TCP/IP, protocols)
  • Concurrency and thread-safety
  • Infrastructure/component design
  • Reliability and fault tolerance
  • Behavioral / team fit

Eligibility

Open — role-based; networking depth varies by team

Your prep plan

  • Solve 40 medium LeetCode problems across core data structures
  • Deeply review OS (processes, threads, locks, memory) and networking (TCP/IP)
  • Practice concurrency problems and reason about deadlock/race conditions
  • Do 2 infrastructure-design mocks emphasizing reliability
  • For C/C++ teams, refresh pointers, memory, and undefined behavior
  • Prepare 5 STAR stories with quantified impact
  • Warm up with 6 medium problems including linked lists and graphs
  • Re-review TCP/IP, threads/locks, and one concurrency pitfall
  • Rehearse one reliability-focused design and your STAR stories

Frequently asked questions

How much networking do I need to know?

It varies by team, but for a networking company, solid TCP/IP and OS fundamentals meaningfully strengthen your candidacy on most infrastructure teams.

Is the coding bar high?

Moderate — medium DSA is typical; fundamentals and systems knowledge often differentiate candidates more than hard algorithms.

Do all roles have a design round?

Not always; it's more common for mid-level and senior infrastructure roles than for entry-level positions.
