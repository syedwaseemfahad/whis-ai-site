Microsoft SWE Interview Guide (2026)

Microsoft's SWE loop in India runs an online coding assessment, then a small set of interviews (usually two coding rounds and, for many roles, a system-design round) culminating in the 'As Appropriate' (AA) round with an experienced interviewer who holds effective veto. Beyond raw coding, Microsoft weighs growth mindset, collaboration and reasoning about reliability at scale.

Round-by-round: exactly what's asked & how to prepare

1

Online Assessment

~60-90 min
DSA coding on a platform

An online coding test (often via Codility/HackerRank or campus platform) with two to three DSA problems focused on data structures and algorithms. Correctness across all test cases and reasonable efficiency gate the interviews.

Example questions
  • Array/string manipulation with efficiency
  • Tree or linked-list operations
  • Graph traversal (BFS/DFS)
  • Dynamic programming basics
  • Hashing and sorting-based problems
How to prepare
  • Solve medium DSA on LeetCode, focus on trees, graphs, linked lists and DP
  • Practice passing all edge-case test cases
  • Time yourself under platform conditions
  • Use NeetCode patterns for structure
Common mistakes
  • Partial solutions failing hidden test cases
  • Ignoring time-limit constraints
  • Skipping DP and graph practice
What they look for
  • All test cases passing
  • Efficient, clean solutions
  • Correct complexity handling
2

Coding Interviews (2 rounds)

~45-60 min each
DSA plus reasoning and light behavioral

Two interviews mixing DSA problem-solving with some fundamentals and behavioral questions. Interviewers watch how you respond to hints, learn from mistakes and communicate, valuing growth mindset alongside correctness. Trees, graphs, linked lists and DP dominate; exotic algorithms are rare.

Example questions
  • Tree/graph problem with follow-up optimization
  • Linked-list manipulation
  • Dynamic programming problem
  • Explaining and improving your approach after a hint
  • A behavioral prompt on teamwork or a past challenge
How to prepare
  • Drill trees, graphs, linked lists and DP with narration
  • Practice taking hints and revising gracefully
  • Prepare a few STAR stories to weave in
  • Do mock coding interviews (Pramp free)
Common mistakes
  • Getting defensive when corrected
  • Silent coding with no communication
  • Over-preparing exotic algorithms while neglecting core patterns
What they look for
  • Growth mindset: learning from hints
  • Clear communication and collaboration
  • Correct, efficient code
3

System Design (many roles)

~45-60 min
Designing a scalable, reliable system

For many roles you get a design round scoped to your level, reasoning about components, data models, APIs, scaling and reliability at scale (Azure/Office/Windows-style platform thinking). You should drive the discussion and surface trade-offs and failure modes.

Example questions
  • Design a scalable service or API
  • Data storage and caching choices
  • Handling high traffic and failures
  • Consistency and reliability trade-offs
  • Monitoring and scaling strategy
How to prepare
  • Study the free System Design Primer on GitHub
  • Learn load balancing, caching, queues, sharding and replication
  • Practice a requirements-first framework
  • Emphasize reliability and failure handling
Common mistakes
  • Ignoring reliability and failure modes
  • No trade-off discussion
  • Designing before scoping requirements
What they look for
  • Requirement-first scoping
  • Reliability-aware design
  • Clear trade-off reasoning
4

As Appropriate (AA) Round

~45-60 min
Holistic final round with veto weight

A senior interviewer (often outside the immediate team) runs a holistic round that can include a harder coding or design problem plus deep behavioral and culture-fit assessment. Their read carries effective veto, so consistency, maturity and growth mindset are decisive.

Example questions
  • A challenging coding or design problem
  • Deep behavioral: conflict, failure, leadership
  • How you learn and partner across roles
  • Reasoning about ambiguity and trade-offs
  • Culture and values alignment
How to prepare
  • Bring your best consistent coding and design form
  • Prepare mature STAR stories on failure and collaboration
  • Practice reasoning calmly through ambiguity
  • Reflect genuinely on how you learn and grow
Common mistakes
  • Treating AA as just another coding round
  • Weak or blame-shifting behavioral answers
  • Losing composure on a hard problem
What they look for
  • Consistent technical strength
  • Mature growth mindset and collaboration
  • Calm reasoning under pressure

What to master

  • Trees, graphs and linked lists
  • Dynamic programming
  • Arrays, strings and hashing
  • Time and space complexity
  • System design fundamentals and reliability
  • Growth-mindset communication
  • Collaboration and cross-role partnering
  • STAR behavioral storytelling

Eligibility

Entry SWE (SDE) roles hire new grads and early-career engineers; system-design depth scales with the role and level. Exact loop composition varies by team and level (flag as evolving). Confirm specifics with your recruiter.

Your prep plan

  • Days 1-4: trees, graphs, linked lists and DP on LeetCode/NeetCode
  • Days 5-6: mixed medium DSA under timed conditions
  • Days 7-8: mock coding interviews with hint-taking practice
  • Days 9-10: system-design fundamentals with one mock
  • Days 11-12: STAR stories emphasizing growth mindset and collaboration
  • Days 13-14: full mock loop including an AA-style holistic round
  • Blitz trees, graphs, linked lists and DP
  • Rehearse STAR stories on failure and teamwork
  • Skim a system-design framework focused on reliability

Frequently asked questions

What is the 'As Appropriate' round?

A holistic final round run by a senior interviewer, often outside the hiring team, whose assessment carries effective veto over the decision. It blends a hard technical problem with deep behavioral evaluation.

How important is growth mindset?

Very. Microsoft explicitly values how you learn from mistakes and respond to hints, not just raw correctness, so stay open and coachable.

Which DSA topics matter most?

Trees, graphs, linked lists and dynamic programming. Exotic algorithms are rarely tested.

Is system design always included?

For many roles yes, scoped to your level, but the exact loop varies by team and seniority.

What platform is the OA on?

Often Codility, HackerRank or a campus platform, with two to three DSA problems. Passing all test cases matters.

How should I handle interviewer hints?

Welcome them, adjust your approach, and explain your revised reasoning. Coachability is a positive signal.
