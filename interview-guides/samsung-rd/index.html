Samsung SRI-B SWE Interview Guide (2026)

SRI-B's process is famous for its Software Competency Test: a single complex problem (often simulation, graph, backtracking, or DP) that must pass roughly 50 hidden test cases within a long time window, usually in C/C++ with restrictions like no STL. Those who clear it move to technical interviews centered on data structures, algorithms, and core CS (OS, DBMS, networks, OOP). A 2026 trend adds AI-literacy questions such as debugging AI-generated code.

Round-by-round: exactly what's asked & how to prepare

1

Software Competency Test

~3+ hours
Single hard problem, all test cases

A proctored, multi-hour coding exam with usually one complex problem judged against ~50 hidden test cases; partial credit is limited, so full correctness matters. Often in C/C++ with constraints (no STL allowed in many variants), demanding careful manual data-structure implementation and edge-case coverage.

Example questions
  • Grid/board simulation with strict rules
  • Graph traversal and shortest path (BFS/DFS/Dijkstra)
  • Backtracking / state-space search
  • Dynamic programming with tight constraints
How to prepare
  • Solve simulation and backtracking problems in C/C++ without STL
  • Practice implementing queues, stacks, and graphs by hand
  • Do long timed problems targeting 100% test-case coverage
  • Free: GeeksforGeeks Samsung/SRI-B practice sets and CodeChef
Common mistakes
  • Depending on STL when it's disallowed
  • Passing sample cases but missing edge/hidden cases
What they look for
  • All hidden test cases passing
  • Robust manual implementation and edge handling
2

Technical Interview 1

~45–60 min
DS/algorithms and coding

A one-on-one round on data structures and algorithms, often asking you to code or dry-run solutions and explain complexity. Interviewers may revisit your competency-test approach or pose fresh medium problems, probing depth on trees, graphs, and recursion.

Example questions
  • Linked list, tree, and graph manipulation
  • Recursion and backtracking
  • Complexity analysis and optimization
  • Array/string algorithmic problems
How to prepare
  • Drill medium DS/algo problems and explain them aloud
  • Be ready to dry-run code on paper/whiteboard
  • Review time/space complexity for common structures
  • Free: GeeksforGeeks DSA course (free tier)
Common mistakes
  • Only giving code without complexity reasoning
  • Struggling to dry-run your own solution
What they look for
  • Clear algorithmic thinking and complexity awareness
  • Correct, explainable code
3

Technical Interview 2

~45–60 min
CS fundamentals (OS, DBMS, networks)

A core-CS round covering operating systems (scheduling, memory, multithreading), DBMS, networking (TCP/IP basics), and OOP, sometimes with C/C++ language internals. For embedded/infra roles this goes deep on systems. A 2026 addition is AI-literacy: debugging or reasoning about AI-generated code.

Example questions
  • Process scheduling, deadlocks, memory management
  • Multithreading and synchronization concepts
  • DBMS normalization, indexing, transactions
  • TCP/IP, OOP principles, and pointers/memory in C
How to prepare
  • Review OS scheduling, memory, and concurrency thoroughly
  • Refresh DBMS and networking fundamentals
  • Practice explaining C/C++ memory and pointers
  • Free: GeeksforGeeks OS/DBMS/CN sections
Common mistakes
  • Neglecting OS/networking, expecting only coding
  • Shallow, memorized definitions without reasoning
What they look for
  • Solid, reasoned CS fundamentals
  • Ability to connect concepts to real systems
4

HR Round

~30–45 min
Fit, stability, motivation

A standard HR conversation on motivation, relocation to Bangalore, long-term goals, and cultural fit, plus logistics and compensation. Samsung looks for stability and genuine interest in R&D work. Expect a few behavioral questions on teamwork and handling pressure.

Example questions
  • Why Samsung R&D and long-term goals
  • Relocation and stability
  • Teamwork and handling deadlines
  • Strengths, weaknesses, and learning attitude
How to prepare
  • Prepare a clear motivation and career-goal narrative
  • Ready 3–4 STAR stories on teamwork and pressure
  • Know Samsung SRI-B's focus areas (AI, IoT, mobile, multimedia)
  • Free: Samsung Research India site for R&D themes
Common mistakes
  • Signaling flight risk or reluctance to relocate
  • Generic motivation with no R&D interest
What they look for
  • Genuine interest and stability
  • Clear communication and fit

What to master

  • Data structures (trees, graphs, linked lists)
  • Algorithms (DP, backtracking, simulation)
  • C/C++ implementation without STL
  • Operating systems (scheduling, memory, threads)
  • DBMS and normalization
  • Computer networks (TCP/IP)
  • OOP concepts
  • AI-code literacy (2026)

Eligibility

Primarily new grad / campus (B.Tech/M.Tech CS/IT); lateral roles use a similar loop

Frequently asked questions

How important is the competency test?

It's the primary filter. You generally must pass most or all ~50 hidden test cases to advance; a great interview cannot rescue a failed test.

Can I use STL or Python in the coding test?

Often no. Many SRI-B variants require C/C++ and disallow STL, so practice implementing data structures by hand. Confirm the exact rules for your test.

Do interviews really test OS and networking?

Yes, core CS fundamentals are heavily tested in the technical rounds, alongside DS/algorithms. Don't prepare coding alone.
