Swiggy Interview Guide (2026)

Swiggy's SWE loop runs 4-6 rounds from its Bangalore HQ: an OA, two-to-three technical rounds spanning DSA (graphs are heavily tested, often rebranded into food-delivery/logistics contexts), a machine-coding/LLD round, and HLD for SDE-2+, capped by a bar-raiser managerial round that blends technical depth with product thinking.

Round-by-round: exactly what's asked & how to prepare

1

Online Assessment

~60-90 min
DSA filter

A timed assessment with roughly 3 medium LeetCode-style problems. Clean, correct solutions with good complexity advance you.

Example questions
  • Array/string medium problems
  • Graph traversal (heavily favored)
  • Hash-map and sorting logic
  • Two-pointer / sliding window
  • Basic DP
How to prepare
  • Drill graphs specifically plus arrays/strings
  • Do timed 3-problem sets
  • Review complexity and edge cases
  • Practice fast, clean coding
Common mistakes
  • Weak on graphs
  • Poor time allocation
  • Failing hidden edge tests
What they look for
  • Correct, efficient solutions
  • Strong graph fluency
  • Clean submissions
2

DSA / Live Coding Round(s)

~45-60 min each
Pattern recognition and clean code

One or two live rounds focused on quickly identifying the right algorithmic pattern and implementing it correctly, with edge cases and complexity. Problems are often framed in delivery/logistics contexts.

Example questions
  • Shortest-path / routing framed as delivery
  • Heap-based scheduling or top-K
  • Sliding window on a stream of orders
  • Tree/graph problem with follow-up
  • Interval or greedy scheduling
How to prepare
  • Master graph algorithms (BFS/DFS, Dijkstra)
  • Practice recognizing patterns fast
  • Rehearse edge-case + complexity closing
  • Do mock live-coding rounds
Common mistakes
  • Slow pattern recognition
  • Skipping edge cases
  • No complexity discussion
What they look for
  • Fast, correct pattern identification
  • Clean implementation
  • Good edge-case handling
3

Machine Coding / LLD

~75-120 min
Build a working system from a spec

You implement a working system from a product requirement, graded on SOLID principles, modularity, naming, and extensibility. Expect to justify design choices.

Example questions
  • Design a splitwise-style expense splitter
  • Cab/ride booking system
  • Snake and ladder or a board game engine
  • An extensible order/notification service
  • Add a feature cleanly to your design
How to prepare
  • Build full OOP systems under a timer (splitwise, cab booking, snake & ladder)
  • Master SOLID and key design patterns
  • Structure into clear modules with interfaces
  • Add basic tests and make it extensible
Common mistakes
  • Incomplete or non-working solution
  • Monolithic, hard-to-extend code
  • Ignoring SOLID and naming
What they look for
  • Working, modular solution
  • Extensible OOP design
  • Clear design justification
4

System Design / HLD (SDE-2+)

~60 min
Swiggy-scale architecture

For SDE-2 and above, an HLD round often anchored in Swiggy's systems: catalog, order dispatch, real-time tracking. You scope, architect, and reason about scale and consistency.

Example questions
  • Design order dispatch / delivery-partner assignment
  • Real-time order tracking
  • Restaurant catalog and search
  • Surge/ETA estimation pipeline
  • Notification and event pipeline
How to prepare
  • Study system-design-primer (free)
  • Practice dispatch/tracking designs
  • Learn queues, streaming, geospatial indexing
  • Do 2 mock designs with estimates
Common mistakes
  • No real-time/latency plan
  • Skipping capacity estimation
  • Ignoring consistency in orders
What they look for
  • Domain-aware, real-time design
  • Sound trade-offs
  • Structured, estimate-driven approach
5

Bar-Raiser / Managerial

~45-60 min
Technical depth + product thinking

A decisive round blending technical depth with product and behavioral judgment, often with a senior engineer or manager. Expect ownership, ambiguity, and product-sense questions alongside project deep-dives.

Example questions
  • A project you owned end-to-end
  • Balancing product trade-offs with engineering
  • Handling ambiguity or shifting priorities
  • A production incident and your response
  • Why Swiggy
How to prepare
  • Prepare 4-5 STAR stories with product angle
  • Ready a strong project deep-dive
  • Practice product-sense reasoning
  • Research Swiggy's business
Common mistakes
  • Pure-tech answers with no product thinking
  • Vague stories
  • No specific motivation
What they look for
  • Blend of technical and product judgment
  • Clear ownership
  • Genuine interest

What to master

  • Graphs (BFS/DFS, shortest path)
  • Arrays, strings, heaps, sliding window
  • DP and greedy
  • OOP, SOLID, design patterns
  • Machine coding / LLD under time
  • Real-time/dispatch system design
  • Queues, streaming, geospatial indexing
  • Product-sense + STAR stories

Eligibility

SDE-1 through SDE-3. LLD/machine coding applies broadly; HLD is added for SDE-2+. Bangalore-headquartered.

Your prep plan

  • Days 1-3: Graph-heavy DSA + arrays/heaps, timed
  • Days 4-5: Live-coding pattern speed
  • Days 6-9: Machine coding: build 3 OOP systems under timer
  • Days 10-11: HLD (dispatch/tracking) + SOLID review
  • Days 12-13: STAR + product-sense stories
  • Day 14: Full mock loop
  • Drill graph mediums + build one machine-coding solution timed
  • Review SOLID/patterns + one dispatch HLD template
  • Prepare 3 STAR stories with a product angle

Frequently asked questions

What DSA topic matters most at Swiggy?

Graphs are the most tested, often rebranded into delivery/logistics contexts. Build strong graph fundamentals.

What is the machine-coding round like?

A 75-120 minute build of a working system from a spec (splitwise, cab booking, snake & ladder), graded on SOLID, modularity, and extensibility.

When does HLD apply?

For SDE-2 and above, anchored in Swiggy's systems like catalog, dispatch, and real-time tracking.

What is the bar-raiser round?

A decisive managerial round mixing technical depth with product thinking and behavioral judgment. Bring product-aware STAR stories.

How many rounds?

Typically 4-6, over roughly three-plus weeks.

How should I practice LLD?

Repeatedly build complete OOP systems under a timer, focusing on clean abstractions, SOLID, and easy extensibility.
