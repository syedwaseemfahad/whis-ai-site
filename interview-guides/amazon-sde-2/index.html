The loop at a glance

Amazon's SDE-2 process is usually an online assessment, then a virtual onsite "loop" of four 55-minute interviews. Every round blends technical work with Leadership Principle questions, and one interviewer is the Bar Raiser — a trained, cross-org interviewer with an effective veto. Details vary by team and location.

StageFormatWhat's tested
Online Assessment~90 min2 coding problems + a design/architecture scenario + a work-style survey
Coding I55 minData structures & algorithms, clean runnable code, edge cases

Round-by-round breakdown

Coding rounds (×2)

What to expect: Two 1:1 live-coding sessions centered on LeetCode-Medium difficulty (some Medium-Hard). Amazon expects syntactically correct, runnable code — not pseudocode — that handles edge cases and is genuinely tested. It is less dynamic-programming-heavy than Google.

Highest-frequency topics

Arrays & strings, hash maps, two pointers, sliding window, trees, graphs (BFS/DFS), heaps, recursion/backtracking, sorting & searching.

Example question themes
  • Merge overlapping meeting intervals and return the free slots.
  • Shortest path for a robot across a warehouse grid, avoiding blocked cells.
  • K closest points to a target using a heap.

Illustrative categories, written by us — not real exam questions.

How to prepare: grind ~120–150 mediums by pattern (two pointers, sliding window, hashing, BFS/DFS, heaps, intervals, trees). Say your brute-force approach out loud, then optimize. Free: NeetCode 150, LeetCode Explore, the "Grind 75" list.

Common mistakes: coding before clarifying constraints, missing edge cases, and untested code. Signals they want: clean decomposition, communication, and testing.

System design

What to expect: SDE-2 gets at least one dedicated design round. Amazon evaluates on practicality, accuracy, efficiency, reliability, optimization, and scalability. You drive the discussion and do the back-of-envelope math.

Example question themes
  • Design an order-tracking service for millions of daily shipments.
  • Design a URL shortener that scales to heavy read traffic.
  • Design a rate limiter for an API gateway.

How to prepare: learn one framework and run it every time — requirements → API → data model → high-level components → scale & bottlenecks (caching, sharding, queues). Free: the "System Design Primer" repo, Hello Interview's free guides.

Common mistakes: never quantifying scale, and skipping trade-offs. State your numbers and defend your choices.

Behavioral & the Bar Raiser

What to expect: a large part of every round is behavioral, mapped to Amazon's 16 Leadership Principles. Expect 2–3 questions per round and 3–5 follow-ups deep. Use STAR with real metrics, and speak to what you personally did. The Bar Raiser digs hardest.

Example question themes
  • A time you disagreed with a decision, then committed anyway.
  • A time you dug into data to find a root cause others missed.
  • A time you took ownership of something outside your remit.

How to prepare: write 12–16 STAR stories with numbers, aiming to cover most of the 16 principles (a strong story covers 2–3). Rehearse the follow-up depth. 2026 note: candidates report at least one question about judicious use of AI tools.

Common mistakes: vague "we did X" stories, no metrics, and answers that collapse under "Dive Deep" follow-ups.

Your prep plan

  • Days 1–7 — Coding volume. 2–3 sessions/day by pattern; verbalize brute-force → optimal; drill edge cases.
  • Days 8–10 — System design. Learn one framework, run 3–4 practice designs with real scale math.
  • Days 11–13 — Leadership Principles. Write and rehearse 12–16 STAR stories with metrics; practice follow-ups.
  • Day 14 — Full mock loop. Simulate all four rounds back to back.
  • Day 1 AM — Top patterns. Two pointers, hashing, BFS/DFS, heaps — 8–10 mediums.
  • Day 1 PM — Stories. 6–8 STAR stories for Customer Obsession, Ownership, Dive Deep, Bias for Action, Deliver Results.
  • Day 2 AM — Design. One framework + one full practice design with scale math.
  • Day 2 PM — Rehearse. Say stories out loud, drill edge cases and clean code.

Frequently asked questions

How many rounds is the Amazon SDE-2 interview?

An online assessment, then a four-to-five interview onsite loop: two coding rounds, at least one system design round, and behavioral rounds on the Leadership Principles — with one Bar Raiser.

Is system design required for SDE-2?

Yes — unlike SDE-1, SDE-2 gets at least one dedicated system design round evaluated on scalability and trade-offs.

How important are the Leadership Principles?

Very. Behavioral is a big part of every round; weak, unquantified stories are a top reason strong engineers get rejected.

How do I prepare in just 2 days?

Use the 2-day crash plan above: top coding patterns, six-plus STAR stories, and one system design framework rehearsed out loud.
