Genpact Interview Guide (2026)

Genpact hires at scale across operations, finance-and-accounting, analytics, and IT tracks, so the exact bar depends heavily on the role you apply for. The common core is a well-rounded aptitude test plus fundamentals for your track (SQL, programming basics, or process/analytics reasoning). Communication, shift flexibility, and stability weigh strongly.

Round-by-round: exactly what's asked & how to prepare

1

Online Assessment

~60–90 min
Aptitude and track fundamentals

A test covering quantitative aptitude, logical reasoning, and verbal ability, plus a role-specific section that may be coding, SQL/Excel, or analytics depending on your track. For voice or support roles a spoken-English test may be added. Difficulty is moderate and pace-driven.

Example questions
  • Quantitative aptitude: percentages, averages, ratios, time-and-work
  • Logical reasoning and data-interpretation sets
  • Verbal ability, grammar, and comprehension
  • Track section: basic coding, SQL, Excel, or analytics reasoning
  • Spoken-English fluency for voice roles (where applicable)
How to prepare
  • Practice timed aptitude and DI sets on PrepInsta or IndiaBix free tiers
  • Sharpen the section for your track (SQL basics, Excel, or coding)
  • Revise grammar and comprehension for the verbal part
  • If a voice role, practice reading passages aloud clearly for the spoken test
Common mistakes
  • Not knowing which track section applies to your role
  • Slow pacing on data-interpretation sets
  • Neglecting the verbal or spoken-English component
What they look for
  • Balanced scores across all sections
  • Solid track-specific fundamentals
  • Clear, confident spoken English where tested
2

Technical / Track Interview

~30–45 min
Track fundamentals and resume

An interview tuned to your track: SQL and programming basics for tech, accounting or process reasoning for F&A, or case and analytics questions for analytics roles. Expect resume, internship, and project questions with practical follow-ups.

Example questions
  • Track core: SQL queries, programming basics, or accounting fundamentals
  • A short case or process-improvement scenario for analytics/ops
  • Walkthrough of an internship or academic project
  • Practical problem-solving in your domain
  • Basic domain concepts relevant to the role
How to prepare
  • Confirm your track and revise its exact fundamentals
  • Practice basic SQL queries or accounting/process concepts as relevant
  • Prepare a clear project or internship story with your contribution
  • Read the job description and align your answers to it
Common mistakes
  • Preparing generic content that misses your track's focus
  • Weak grasp of basics like SQL joins or core accounting terms
  • Vague project descriptions without your specific role
What they look for
  • Accurate track fundamentals
  • Applied, practical reasoning on a scenario
  • Clear ownership of your project or internship work
3

HR Interview

~15–30 min
Fit, shifts, and stability

A behavioral round on motivation, communication, relocation, shift and rotational-timing flexibility, and long-term intent. For an operations-heavy firm, genuine flexibility and stability are decisive.

Example questions
  • Why Genpact and what you know about the company
  • Comfort with night shifts, rotational timings, and relocation
  • Self-introduction, strengths, and weaknesses
  • A STAR story on teamwork or handling pressure
  • Career goals and reasons you will stay
How to prepare
  • Read Genpact's business areas and recent India context briefly
  • Decide your honest stance on shifts and relocation beforehand
  • Prepare one STAR story on teamwork or pressure
  • Rehearse a concise self-introduction
Common mistakes
  • Hesitating on shift or relocation flexibility
  • Generic reasons for choosing the company
  • An unstructured, overlong self-introduction
What they look for
  • Genuine flexibility on shifts and location
  • Confident, clear communication
  • Authentic, specific behavioral answers

What to master

  • Quantitative aptitude
  • Logical reasoning
  • Verbal & communication
  • SQL basics
  • Programming/analytics basics
  • Accounting/process (F&A tracks)
  • Case reasoning
  • Behavioral fit

Eligibility

Typically graduates with ~60% / 6.0 CGPA; branch and track requirements vary widely by role and drive.

Your prep plan

  • Days 1–4: build timed aptitude, reasoning, and DI speed
  • Days 5–7: master your track fundamentals (SQL, Excel, coding, or accounting)
  • Days 8–9: revise verbal and, if relevant, practice spoken English aloud
  • Days 10–11: prepare project/internship stories with your specific role
  • Days 12–13: run a full mock assessment for pacing
  • Day 14: rehearse HR answers on shifts, relocation, and motivation
  • Do two timed aptitude sets and revise your track basics
  • Prepare one project story and a track-specific concept refresh
  • Rehearse self-intro plus honest shift and relocation answers

Frequently asked questions

Does the process change by track?

Substantially. The assessment's technical section and the interview focus differ for operations, F&A, analytics, and IT roles. Confirm your track and prepare accordingly.

Is there a spoken-English test?

For voice or customer-support roles, a spoken-English assessment may apply. Practice reading and speaking clearly if your role is voice-facing.

How important is shift flexibility?

Very. Many Genpact roles involve rotational or night shifts, so genuine flexibility and stability strongly influence the HR decision.
