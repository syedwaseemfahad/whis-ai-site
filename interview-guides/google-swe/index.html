Google SWE Interview Guide (2026)

A first-principles coding loop that rewards clear reasoning over memorized patterns.

The hiring process

One or two technical phone screens (medium/hard coding in a shared doc), then a virtual onsite of 4–6 rounds mixing coding, code comprehension, a Googleyness round, and (L5+) system design, ending with a central hiring committee and team match.

RoundFormatWhat's tested
Phone screen~45 mincoding/DSA in a shared doc, spoken reasoning, complexity analysis
Coding (x2–4)~45 min eachDSA: arrays, strings, trees/graphs, DP, recursion
Code comprehension~45–60 minreading, debugging and optimizing existing code (2026 pilot adds AI-assisted variants for some junior/mid roles)
System design~45 minHLD for L5+; scalability, data modeling, trade-offs
Googleyness & Leadership~45 minbehavioral, collaboration, ambiguity, values

Process and cutoffs vary by drive and change over time — always confirm on the official careers page.

What to master

  • graphs (BFS/DFS, shortest path)
  • dynamic programming
  • trees and recursion
  • hash maps and two pointers
  • system design for L5+
  • spoken problem-solving and complexity

Eligibility

open — role/level-based (L3 new grad through L5+), not CGPA-gated

Your prep plan

2-week plan

  • Days 1–4: drill arrays, strings, hashing and two pointers; solve out loud while writing complexity
  • Days 5–8: go deep on trees, graphs (BFS/DFS, topological, Dijkstra) and recursion
  • Days 9–11: dynamic programming patterns (knapsack, intervals, grid, LIS)
  • Days 12–13: practice reading and debugging unfamiliar code; if targeting L5+, do 2–3 system design mocks
  • Day 14: full timed mock in a plain doc, narrating your approach end to end

2-day crash plan

  • Day 1: 8–10 medium graph and DP problems, each explained aloud with complexity
  • Day 2: one hard problem, one debug-existing-code drill, and 3 Googleyness stories in STAR form

FAQ

How much do graphs and DP really matter?

A lot. Google leans heavily on graph traversal and dynamic programming; being fluent there is one of the biggest levers on your outcome.

What is the new code comprehension round?

Announced as a 2026 pilot for select junior/mid US teams, it asks you to read, debug and optimize an existing codebase, sometimes with an AI assistant, testing prompting and output validation. Availability is limited, so confirm with your recruiter.

Do I pick my team before or after interviews?

Usually after. You pass a general loop and hiring committee first, then go through team matching, which can add time to the timeline.
