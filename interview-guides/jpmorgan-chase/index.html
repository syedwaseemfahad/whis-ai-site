JPMorgan Chase Interview Guide (2026)

JPMorgan is one of the largest technology employers in finance, so its engineering interviews reward dependable fundamentals and clear collaboration more than exotic algorithm tricks. Expect data structures, some object-oriented and design questions, and heavy behavioral evaluation against the firm's leadership and business principles. The bar is approachable for prepared candidates but broad, and communication carries real weight throughout.

Round-by-round: exactly what's asked & how to prepare

1

Online Assessment / Coding Game

~60–90 min
Aptitude and coding fundamentals

For early-career, a gamified cognitive assessment measuring attention, memory, and reasoning, sometimes paired with a HackerRank coding stage. Experienced tracks get more direct algorithm questions. There are no single right answers on the game portion, so consistency and focus matter.

Example questions
  • Pattern recognition and short-term memory tasks
  • Numerical and logical reasoning
  • Easy-to-medium coding: arrays, strings, hashing
  • Simple simulation or parsing problems
  • Careful reading of problem constraints
How to prepare
  • Try free gamified-assessment practice samples
  • Solve easy/medium LeetCode on arrays and strings
  • Practice numerical reasoning on free aptitude sites
  • Rest well; the cognitive game rewards focus over cramming
Common mistakes
  • Overthinking the game portion looking for a trick
  • Neglecting self-tests on the coding section
What they look for
  • Consistent, focused performance across game tasks
  • Correct, tested coding submissions
2

HireVue Behavioral (recorded)

~20–30 min
Motivation and values fit

Recorded answers to behavioral and situational prompts, often mapped to JPMorgan's business principles. Short prep and answer timers, usually no re-records. They want specific reasons for the firm and evidence of teamwork, integrity, and resilience.

Example questions
  • Why JPMorgan and why this line of business
  • A time you collaborated across a disagreement
  • Handling a setback or an ambiguous problem
  • Living a value like integrity or customer focus
How to prepare
  • Read the firm's business principles and map stories to each
  • Draft and rehearse 6–8 STAR answers on camera
  • Learn the specific team or program you applied to
  • Practice tight 60–90 second recorded responses
Common mistakes
  • Boilerplate why-JPMorgan with no specifics
  • Long, unstructured stories that miss the result
What they look for
  • Concrete alignment with stated values
  • Clear, concise on-camera communication
3

Technical Interview

~45 min
DSA, OOP, and applied engineering

Live coding and technical discussion with engineers. Expect medium DSA, object-oriented design, questions on databases and APIs, and a walkthrough of your projects. For newer stacks they may probe language and framework basics. Reasoning and clean code beat brute cleverness.

Example questions
  • Arrays, strings, hashing, and basic trees
  • Object-oriented design of a small feature
  • REST/API and database fundamentals
  • Deep-dive on a project you built
  • Debugging and edge-case handling
How to prepare
  • Solve LeetCode mediums plus a few OOP design katas
  • Review SQL joins, indexing, and basic API design
  • Prepare a clear architecture story for your best project
  • Use free CS fundamentals refreshers (MIT OCW, freeCodeCamp)
Common mistakes
  • Ignoring readability and naming while coding
  • Being unable to explain your own project's design
What they look for
  • Clean, maintainable code with clear reasoning
  • Solid grasp of practical engineering basics
4

Final / Superday Fit

~30–45 min
Culture fit and ownership

Conversational rounds with senior engineers or managers on teamwork, motivation, and how you handle pressure and ambiguity. May include lighter technical follow-ups. They assess whether you will collaborate well and grow within a large organization.

Example questions
  • Handling shifting priorities on a project
  • Giving or receiving difficult feedback
  • Long-term goals and fit with the team
  • Curiosity about fintech and the firm's products
How to prepare
  • Prepare stories on adaptability and feedback
  • Research the team's products and tech stack
  • Prepare 3 sharp questions for interviewers
  • Review the JD and match your experience point by point
Common mistakes
  • Showing no interest in the specific team's work
  • Underselling teamwork in favor of solo heroics
What they look for
  • Genuine collaboration mindset and humility
  • Clear motivation to build in fintech

What to master

  • Data structures and algorithms
  • Object-oriented design
  • SQL and databases
  • APIs and web fundamentals
  • System design basics
  • Behavioral / business principles
  • Cognitive/aptitude reasoning
  • Your resume and projects

Eligibility

Role-based: analyst and grad programs target students and recent graduates; experienced software roles require relevant engineering background. Degree and visa requirements vary by region and program.

Frequently asked questions

Is the coding bar hard?

It is moderate. Reliable medium-level DSA plus clean, well-explained code is usually enough; exotic hard problems are uncommon.

What is the gamified assessment about?

It measures cognitive traits like memory and reasoning with no single right answer. Stay focused and answer honestly rather than gaming it.

How much do behavioral rounds count?

A lot. JPMorgan weighs values fit and teamwork heavily, so prepare specific, structured stories.
