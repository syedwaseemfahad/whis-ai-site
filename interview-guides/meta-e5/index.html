Meta E5 Interview Guide (2026)

Meta's E5 (senior SWE) loop is a high-bar process: after a recruiter screen and a technical phone screen, the onsite typically runs two coding rounds (one may be an AI-enabled format in 2026), a system-design (or product-architecture) round, and a behavioral round led by a loop lead. Coding is fast, DP is generally excluded, and failing behavioral is an automatic no-hire.

Round-by-round: exactly what's asked & how to prepare

1

Recruiter Screen

~20-30 min
Level fit, timeline and logistics

A recruiter confirms your target level (E5 senior), background, timeline and location, and outlines the loop. As of 2026, some E5+ candidates report an AI-native prescreener path instead of the classic call (flag as evolving).

Example questions
  • Background and E5-level justification
  • Timeline and location
  • Loop format and expectations
  • Compensation basics
How to prepare
  • Be able to justify E5-level scope and impact
  • Know your resume and biggest projects cold
  • Prepare a crisp senior-level pitch
  • Ask about loop format and AI-enabled rounds
Common mistakes
  • Under-selling senior scope
  • Unclear timeline
  • No questions
What they look for
  • Clear senior-level framing
  • Genuine interest
  • Logistical readiness
2

Technical Phone Screen

~45 min
Fast DSA coding in CoderPad

About 5 minutes of resume chat then ~35 minutes of coding: two easy-to-medium LeetCode-style problems in CoderPad with execution off. Speed and clean communication matter; you should solve both comfortably. This gates the onsite.

Example questions
  • Two easy-medium array/string/hashmap problems
  • Efficiency and complexity reasoning
  • Edge-case handling
  • Fast, clean implementation
  • Concise verbal narration
How to prepare
  • Practice Meta-tagged LeetCode problems for speed
  • Solve two problems in 40 minutes as a drill
  • Code without execution to build accuracy
  • Narrate approach and complexity crisply
Common mistakes
  • Solving only one problem in the time
  • Buggy code from not dry-running (execution is off)
  • Over-clarifying and wasting minutes
What they look for
  • Both problems solved cleanly and fast
  • Correct complexity
  • Clear, efficient communication
3

Onsite Coding (2 rounds, one may be AI-enabled)

~45-60 min
Fast DSA plus, in 2026, an AI-assisted multi-file problem

Traditional rounds give roughly two easy-medium problems per 45 minutes with execution off and DP generally banned. In 2026 one round may be a 60-minute AI-enabled multi-part problem (bug fix, implement, optimize) in a multi-file IDE with access to models like GPT or Claude, testing how well you direct and validate AI.

Example questions
  • Two easy-medium problems (graphs, hashing, two pointers)
  • Multi-part problem: fix a bug, implement, then optimize (AI round)
  • Directing an AI assistant precisely and validating output
  • Complexity and edge-case reasoning
  • Clean code across multiple files
How to prepare
  • Drill Meta-tagged easy-medium problems for speed and accuracy
  • Note DP is generally excluded; prioritize graphs, hashing, heaps
  • Practice an AI-assisted coding session: prompt, validate, correct
  • Practice reading and editing multi-file code
Common mistakes
  • Being too slow for the two-problem pace
  • Blindly trusting AI output in the AI round
  • Buggy code from skipping the dry-run
What they look for
  • Fast, correct solutions
  • Effective, validated AI use
  • Clean multi-file edits
4

System Design / Product Architecture

~45-60 min
Scalable design at senior depth

Required from E4 up and central at E5: infra roles get system design, product roles get product architecture. You must scope requirements, design components, data models and APIs, and drive deep trade-off discussion on scale, consistency and failure at senior depth.

Example questions
  • Design a scalable feed, messaging or notification system
  • Product architecture for a user-facing feature
  • Data model, API and storage choices
  • Sharding, caching and consistency trade-offs
  • Handling scale, hotspots and failures
How to prepare
  • Study the System Design Primer and Meta-style design walkthroughs
  • Practice a requirements-first framework with deep dives
  • Prepare both infra and product-architecture angles
  • Do timed mock design sessions
Common mistakes
  • Staying shallow at a senior-level round
  • No trade-off or failure discussion
  • Not driving the conversation
What they look for
  • Senior-depth scoping and deep dives
  • Strong trade-off reasoning
  • Ownership of the discussion
5

Behavioral (Loop Lead)

~45 min
Senior impact, collaboration and, in 2026, AI usage

Led by the loop lead who aggregates all feedback, this round probes senior-level impact, conflict, driving projects, mentorship and, as of 2026, how you use AI in your work. Failing this round is an automatic no-hire, so it is decisive.

Example questions
  • Driving a large project end to end
  • Resolving conflict or cross-team disagreement
  • Mentoring or leveling up others
  • Handling ambiguity and setting direction
  • How you use AI tools responsibly and effectively
How to prepare
  • Prepare 8-10 STAR stories at senior scope and impact
  • Include conflict, mentorship, ambiguity and cross-team stories
  • Prepare a genuine AI-usage narrative
  • Practice concise, high-signal delivery
Common mistakes
  • Junior-scoped stories for a senior round
  • Vague impact with no metrics
  • Weak or evasive AI-usage answers
What they look for
  • Clear senior-level ownership and impact
  • Mature conflict and mentorship handling
  • Thoughtful, honest AI usage

What to master

  • Fast easy-medium DSA (graphs, hashing, heaps, two pointers)
  • Note: DP generally excluded
  • AI-assisted multi-file coding: prompting and validation
  • System design (infra)
  • Product architecture (product roles)
  • Scaling, consistency and failure trade-offs
  • Senior-scope STAR behavioral stories
  • Responsible AI usage narratives

Eligibility

E5 is senior SWE; system design is required from E4 up and central at E5. As of August 2026 some E5+ candidates report an AI-native prescreener and screener path replacing parts of the classic funnel (flag as evolving). Behavioral is a hard gate. Confirm loop specifics with your recruiter.

Your prep plan

  • Days 1-4: Meta-tagged easy-medium DSA for speed (graphs, hashing, heaps)
  • Days 5-6: two-problems-in-40-minutes timed drills, execution off
  • Days 7-8: one AI-assisted multi-file coding practice session
  • Days 9-11: system design and product architecture with timed mocks
  • Days 12-13: 8-10 senior-scope STAR stories plus an AI-usage narrative
  • Day 14: full mock loop including behavioral
  • Speed-drill Meta-tagged problems (skip DP)
  • Rehearse 8 senior STAR stories plus AI-usage answer
  • One system-design or product-architecture mock

Frequently asked questions

Is dynamic programming tested at Meta?

Generally no. Meta's traditional coding rounds usually exclude DP; focus on graphs, hashing, heaps, two pointers and fast implementation.

What is the AI-enabled coding round?

A 2026 format: a 60-minute multi-part problem (bug fix, implement, optimize) in a multi-file IDE with access to models like GPT or Claude. It tests how well you prompt, validate and correct AI output.

Can I be rejected on behavioral alone?

Yes. Failing the behavioral round is an automatic no-hire, and the loop lead aggregates all feedback, so it is decisive.

How fast is the coding pace?

Very fast: about two easy-medium problems per 45 minutes with code execution turned off, so accuracy without running the code is essential.

Is system design required at E5?

Yes. Design is required from E4 up; E5 gets system design (infra) or product architecture (product roles) at senior depth.

What is the AI-native screener path?

As of August 2026 some senior candidates skip the classic recruiter call and CodeSignal OA for an AI-native prescreener questionnaire and a 60-minute AI-native screener before the onsite. It is still evolving.
