PolicyBazaar Interview Guide (2026)

PolicyBazaar runs insurance quote engines, comparison flows, and integrations with dozens of insurers, so interviews emphasize solid DSA fundamentals, low-level design, and strong CS basics over exotic algorithms. Rounds skew practical and move quickly. Interviewers look for engineers who write clean code, model systems sensibly, and know their fundamentals cold.

Round-by-round: exactly what's asked & how to prepare

1

Coding Round

~60–90 min
DSA fundamentals screen

One coding round (sometimes several problems) drawn from core topics like arrays and linked lists, often with a puzzle or two. Difficulty is easy-to-medium with an emphasis on correct, clean implementation. Campus loops may pack more problems into fewer rounds.

Example questions
  • Array and linked-list manipulation
  • Basic string processing
  • Simple recursion / backtracking
  • Logic puzzles and math
  • Hashmap frequency counting
How to prepare
  • Drill array, string, and linked-list easies-to-mediums (leetcode.com)
  • Practice common logic puzzles
  • Review recursion templates
  • Follow Striver's SDE sheet basics (takeuforward.org)
Common mistakes
  • Overthinking easy problems
  • Pointer bugs on linked lists
  • Skipping edge cases
What they look for
  • Correct, clean solutions
  • Sound complexity reasoning
  • Solid fundamentals
2

Technical – Developer Round

~45–60 min
Coding plus fundamentals

A senior developer runs medium DSA plus questions on CS fundamentals — OOP, DBMS, and language internals. You explain your approach and defend your fundamentals. Practical correctness matters more than clever tricks.

Example questions
  • Medium array/tree/DP problems
  • OOP concept application
  • SQL query and indexing questions
  • Language-specific internals (GC, collections)
  • Time/space optimization follow-ups
How to prepare
  • Solve 15 medium DSA problems (neetcode.io)
  • Revise OOP, DBMS, and SQL fundamentals
  • Practice explaining language internals
  • Do a couple of peer mocks
Common mistakes
  • Weak DBMS/SQL answers
  • Coding before explaining
  • Shaky fundamentals under follow-up
What they look for
  • Clean code with clear reasoning
  • Strong CS fundamentals
  • Good optimization instincts
3

Team Lead / LLD Round

~45–60 min
Design and CS depth

A team lead covers low-level design (a classic like LRU cache) plus a discussion of your projects, experience, and CS fundamentals. Expect to reason about class structure and trade-offs. This round weighs practical design judgment.

Example questions
  • Implement an LRU / LFU cache
  • Design a quote-comparison module
  • OOP model for an insurance-policy catalog
  • Rate limiter or retry handler for insurer APIs
  • Notification / reminder scheduler
How to prepare
  • Implement LRU/LFU and 2–3 other LLD problems (refactoring.guru)
  • Revise SOLID and design patterns
  • Review the low-level-design-primer (github.com/prasadgujar)
  • Be ready to defend past-project decisions
Common mistakes
  • Not clarifying requirements before designing
  • Tightly coupled classes
  • Vague answers on your own projects
What they look for
  • Clean, correct LLD implementation
  • Sensible OOP structure
  • Depth on your own work
4

Leadership / HR Round

~30–45 min
Fit, ownership, and CTO/lead sync

A conversation with senior leadership (sometimes the CTO) plus HR on projects, motivation, and culture fit. They probe ownership, communication, and why insurtech. Expect straightforward behavioral questions and offer discussion.

Example questions
  • A project you owned end to end
  • Handling a tight deadline or incident
  • A disagreement you navigated
  • Working across teams / with insurers
  • Why PolicyBazaar / fintech
How to prepare
  • Prepare 5 STAR stories with impact
  • Research PolicyBazaar's business and scale
  • Prepare questions for leadership
  • Practice concise story delivery
Common mistakes
  • Vague, unquantified stories
  • No specific reason for the company
  • Poor conflict framing
What they look for
  • Clear ownership and impact
  • Good communication
  • Genuine domain interest

What to master

  • Arrays & strings
  • Linked lists & recursion
  • Trees & DP basics
  • Low-level design / OOP
  • DBMS & SQL
  • System design basics
  • Language internals
  • Caching (LRU/LFU)

How the package is structured

  • Structure is fixed base + variable + joining bonus + ESOP that grows with level.
  • As a listed company (PB Fintech), ask about RSU/ESOP vesting and current share price to value equity.
  • Cash base is the most negotiable lever with a competing offer in hand.
  • Getting placed at the right level matters more than round-level negotiation.

Your prep plan

  • Days 1–3: arrays, strings, and linked lists to medium level
  • Days 4–6: trees, light DP, and a timed coding test
  • Days 7–8: revise OOP, DBMS, and SQL fundamentals
  • Days 9–11: implement LRU/LFU and two more LLD problems
  • Day 12: five STAR stories and a project walkthrough
  • Days 13–14: mixed mocks and fundamentals review
  • Re-solve core array, linked-list, and tree patterns
  • Implement LRU cache from scratch and skim SQL/DBMS notes
  • Lock in STAR stories and your 'why PolicyBazaar' answer

Frequently asked questions

How algorithm-heavy is the loop?

It's moderate — solid fundamentals, LLD, and CS basics matter more than hard competitive-programming problems.

Are CS fundamentals actually tested?

Yes, DBMS/SQL, OOP, and language internals come up in the developer and lead rounds, so revise them.

How fast is the process?

It's one of the quicker loops — offers can come within roughly a week to two of the first round.
